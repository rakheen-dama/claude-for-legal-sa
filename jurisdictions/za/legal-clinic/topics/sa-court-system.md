# SA Court System — South African Framework

This overlay covers the South African court hierarchy, jurisdictional limits, caption formats, case number conventions, and court calendar rules. It is loaded by court-navigation, document-drafting, and jurisdiction-check skills when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Constitutional framework

The courts of South Africa are established and governed by Chapter 8 of the Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996. Section 165 vests judicial authority in the courts, which are independent and subject only to the Constitution and the law.

The court structure is arranged in a hierarchy, with the Constitutional Court at the apex. Each court has defined jurisdiction — original, appeal, or both — and a litigant must approach the correct court or risk the matter being dismissed for want of jurisdiction.

---

## 2. Court hierarchy

### 2.1 Constitutional Court (Constitution s167)

The Constitutional Court is the highest court in the Republic on all constitutional matters. It may decide:

- Any constitutional matter
- Any issue connected with a decision on a constitutional matter
- Any other matter if granted leave to appeal

The Constitutional Court consists of the Chief Justice, the Deputy Chief Justice, and nine other judges. It sits in Johannesburg (Constitution Square). Leave to appeal to the Constitutional Court is required for all matters except those coming directly from the High Court or Supreme Court of Appeal where the Constitution so requires.

A matter is a **constitutional matter** if it involves the interpretation, protection, or enforcement of the Constitution. Disputes purely about common law or statute with no constitutional dimension are not constitutional matters and may not be heard in the Constitutional Court.

### 2.2 Supreme Court of Appeal (SCA) (Constitution s168)

The Supreme Court of Appeal is the highest court of appeal in South Africa on all matters **other than constitutional matters**. It hears appeals from the High Court (including the Full Bench) and may, in appropriate cases, hear appeals on constitutional matters not reserved for the Constitutional Court alone.

The SCA sits in Bloemfontein. Appeal to the SCA requires leave, which may be granted by the SCA itself or the High Court.

### 2.3 High Court

The High Court consists of nine divisions, each serving one or more provinces:

| Division | Seat | Province(s) served |
|---|---|---|
| Gauteng Division (Pretoria) | Pretoria (main); Johannesburg (local) | Gauteng |
| Western Cape Division | Cape Town | Western Cape |
| Eastern Cape Division | Bhisho (main); Grahamstown; Port Elizabeth | Eastern Cape |
| KwaZulu-Natal Division | Pietermaritzburg (main); Durban (local) | KwaZulu-Natal |
| Free State Division | Bloemfontein | Free State |
| Limpopo Division | Thohoyandou (main); Polokwane | Limpopo |
| Mpumalanga Division | Mbombela | Mpumalanga |
| North West Division | Mahikeng | North West |
| Northern Cape Division | Kimberley | Northern Cape |

The High Court has **unlimited civil jurisdiction** and hears matters involving amounts above the Magistrates' Court jurisdiction ceiling (currently R400,000 for Regional Courts) and matters that Magistrates' Courts are prohibited from hearing (e.g., divorces, wills, mental capacity declarations, rehabilitation orders).

The High Court also has **appeal jurisdiction** over Magistrates' Court decisions. A Full Bench of the High Court (typically three judges) hears appeals from a single High Court judge.

#### High Court original jurisdiction — notable matters

1. **Divorce** — all divorce matters are heard in the High Court, regardless of the quantum of assets (Divorce Act 70 of 1979).
2. **Mental capacity / curator bonis** — applications to declare a person mentally incapacitated and to appoint a curator.
3. **Insolvency sequestration** — voluntary and compulsory sequestration applications (Insolvency Act 24 of 1936).
4. **Winding-up of companies** — compulsory winding-up under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 s81.
5. **Interdicts** — urgent interdicts are routinely brought in the High Court on the motion roll.
6. **Review of administrative action** — subject to the 180-day rule under PAJA s7.
7. **Constitutional relief** — declaration of invalidity of legislation.

### 2.4 Magistrates' Court

Magistrates' Courts are the workhorse of the civil and criminal justice system. They are governed by the Magistrates' Courts Act 32 of 1944 (MCA) and the Rules Regulating Proceedings in the Magistrates' Courts (the **Magistrates' Courts Rules** or **MCR**).

Two tiers exist:

#### District Magistrates' Court

- **Civil jurisdiction:** Claims up to R200,000 (increased from 27 March 2014 by Government Gazette 37477).
- **Prohibited matters:** Cannot hear divorces, matters concerning the validity of wills, matters concerning the status of mentally ill persons, or matters concerning rehabilitation of insolvents.
- **Criminal jurisdiction:** Regional courts handle serious crimes. District courts handle summary offences and lesser crimes.

#### Regional Magistrates' Court

- **Civil jurisdiction:** R200,001–R400,000 (MCA s29).
- **Can hear:** Divorces, maintenance matters, customary marriage dissolution.
- **Criminal jurisdiction:** All offences except treason and murder (original jurisdiction to impose imprisonment up to 15 years and fines up to R120,000).

### 2.5 Small Claims Court

The Small Claims Court is established under the Small Claims Courts Act 61 of 1984. Key features:

- **Jurisdiction:** Up to R20,000 (increased periodically by notice in the Gazette).
- **Natural persons only** — juristic persons (companies, close corporations) may not sue in the Small Claims Court; they may be sued (Small Claims Courts Act s7).
- **No legal representation** — parties must appear in person; attorneys may not appear on behalf of parties.
- **Magistrate sits as commissioner** — no pleadings, no formal process — parties appear and explain their claim.
- **No appeal to High Court** on facts; only special review for gross irregularity.

## 3. Specialist courts

South Africa has a range of specialist courts that exercise exclusive or concurrent jurisdiction in defined areas:

| Court | Enabling legislation | Subject matter |
|---|---|---|
| Labour Court | Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 s151 | Unfair labour practices, dismissal disputes above earnings threshold, strikes, interdicts |
| Labour Appeal Court | LRA s167 | Appeals from Labour Court |
| Land Claims Court | Restitution of Land Rights Act 22 of 1994 | Land restitution claims, evictions (PIE Act), land reform |
| Equality Court | Promotion of Equality and Prevention of Unfair Discrimination Act 4 of 2000 s16 | Unfair discrimination, hate speech, harassment — sits at every Magistrates' Court |
| Competition Tribunal | Competition Act 89 of 1998 s26 | Prohibited practices, mergers, exemptions |
| Competition Appeal Court | Competition Act s36 | Appeals from Competition Tribunal |
| Tax Court | Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011 s116 | Tax disputes between taxpayers and SARS |
| Water Tribunal | National Water Act 36 of 1998 s148 | Appeals against decisions of the Water Tribunal |
| Children's Court | Children's Act 38 of 2005 s45 | Care and protection of children; every Magistrates' Court is a Children's Court |
| Maintenance Court | Maintenance Act 99 of 1998 | Maintenance orders; every Magistrates' Court is a Maintenance Court |

**Equality Court practical note:** A complainant may bring a discrimination complaint to the Equality Court at any Magistrates' Court. The presiding officer is a magistrate. In cases involving prohibited grounds under PEPUDA, the respondent bears the burden of proving that the discrimination was fair once an adverse impact is shown.

**Children's Court practical note:** A social worker or child may approach any Children's Court for an order in terms of Children's Act s150. The court may make a temporary safe care order, a child protection order, or transfer the matter to the High Court if the matter warrants it.

## 4. Jurisdiction limits — summary table

| Court | Civil jurisdiction | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Small Claims Court | Up to R20,000 | Natural persons only; no legal representation (Small Claims Courts Act s7) |

| Regional Magistrates' Court | R200,001–R400,000 | Can hear divorces, maintenance, customary marriage dissolution |
| High Court | Unlimited | Original and appeal jurisdiction; exclusive on divorce, wills, insolvency |
| Labour Court | — (specialist) | Exclusive jurisdiction over LRA matters |
| Land Claims Court | — (specialist) | Exclusive jurisdiction over restitution and PIE evictions |
| Children's Court | As per Magistrates' Court | Every Magistrates' Court is a Children's Court (Children's Act s45) |
| Equality Court | As per Magistrates' Court | Every Magistrates' Court is an Equality Court (PEPUDA s16) |

## 5. SA court caption formats

South African court documents use a distinct caption style. There is no "v." between parties in the traditional common-law sense — SA uses "and" with a spacing layout. The party designation (Applicant/Respondent or Plaintiff/Defendant) appears flush right.

### 5.1 Magistrates' Court caption

```
IN THE MAGISTRATE'S COURT FOR THE DISTRICT OF JOHANNESBURG
HELD AT JOHANNESBURG

Case No: [YEAR]/[NUMBER]

In the matter between:

[APPLICANT/PLAINTIFF NAME]                    Applicant/Plaintiff

and

[RESPONDENT/DEFENDANT NAME]                   Respondent/Defendant
```

**Notes:**
- The district name appears in the header (e.g., "DISTRICT OF JOHANNESBURG", "DISTRICT OF CAPE TOWN").
- The venue appears on the second line ("HELD AT JOHANNESBURG").
- Regional Courts use: "IN THE REGIONAL COURT FOR THE REGIONAL DIVISION OF [REGION] HELD AT [VENUE]".
- Multiple parties: second and subsequent parties are numbered ("First Respondent", "Second Respondent").

### 5.2 High Court caption

```
IN THE HIGH COURT OF SOUTH AFRICA
GAUTENG DIVISION, JOHANNESBURG

Case No: [YEAR]/[NUMBER]

In the matter between:

[APPLICANT NAME]                              Applicant

and

[RESPONDENT NAME]                             Respondent
```

**Division names in captions:**
- Western Cape: "WESTERN CAPE DIVISION, CAPE TOWN"
- KwaZulu-Natal (Durban): "KWAZULU-NATAL LOCAL DIVISION, DURBAN"
- KwaZulu-Natal (PMB): "KWAZULU-NATAL DIVISION, PIETERMARITZBURG"
- Eastern Cape (Port Elizabeth): "EASTERN CAPE LOCAL DIVISION, PORT ELIZABETH"

### 5.3 Labour Court caption

```
IN THE LABOUR COURT OF SOUTH AFRICA
JOHANNESBURG

Case No: J[NUMBER]/[YEAR]

In the matter between:

[APPLICANT NAME]                              Applicant

and

[RESPONDENT NAME]                             Respondent
```

Labour Court case numbers are prefixed with "J" (Johannesburg), "C" (Cape Town), "D" (Durban), or "P" (Port Elizabeth).

### 5.4 Urgent applications

Urgent applications include a heading on the Notice of Motion:

```
URGENT APPLICATION

IN THE HIGH COURT OF SOUTH AFRICA
GAUTENG DIVISION, JOHANNESBURG
```

The certificate of urgency (a short affidavit explaining the urgency) must be filed with the notice of motion. The applicant's attorney must attend the duty judge at the given time and obtain a date for the urgent hearing.

## 6. Case number formats

Case number conventions vary slightly by division but follow a general pattern:

| Court | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Magistrates' Court (most) | YYYY/NNNNNN | 2025/012345 |
| High Court Gauteng | YYYY/NNNNN | 2025/01234 |
| High Court Western Cape | YYYY/NNNNN | 2025/01234 |
| Labour Court | J[N]/YYYY or JR[N]/YYYY | J123/2025; JR456/2025 |
| Constitutional Court | CCT[N]/YYYY | CCT12/2025 |
| SCA | [N]/YYYY | 456/2025 |

**JR numbers** in the Labour Court are used for review applications (judicial review of arbitration awards). Regular matters use J numbers.

## 7. Court terms, recess, and operating hours

### 7.1 Magistrates' Courts

**December–January recess:** The Magistrates' Courts Rules Rule 21B excludes the days between 16 December and 15 January (inclusive) from the calculation of any period of time prescribed in the Rules for the delivery of a pleading or document. A deadline falling within the recess extends to the first business day after 15 January.

**Practical effect:** A plea that would otherwise be due on 20 December is not due until the court re-opens after 15 January. Attorneys must calendar this carefully to avoid defaults.

**Court hours:**
- Court offices open: 08:00–16:00
- Filing accepted: 08:00–15:00 (documents lodged after 15:00 are typically diarised the following day)
- Closed: Weekends and public holidays

### 7.2 High Court terms

The High Court operates in four terms:

| Term | Approximate dates |
|---|---|
| First term | 1 February – Thursday before Easter |
| Second term | First Monday after Easter + 2 weeks – end June |
| Third term | Mid-July – end September |
| Fourth term | Mid-October – end November |

The exact term dates are published annually in each division's practice directives. Urgent matters and applications may be heard in recess by the duty judge.

### 7.3 SA public holidays affecting court deadlines

South Africa has 12 public holidays declared under the Public Holidays Act 36 of 1994. Courts are closed and time does not run (for purposes of the Magistrates' Courts Rules Rule 1(2)) on:

| Date | Holiday |
|---|---|
| 1 January | New Year's Day |
| 21 March | Human Rights Day |
| Good Friday | Variable |
| Family Day (Easter Monday) | Variable |
| 27 April | Freedom Day |
| 1 May | Workers' Day |
| 16 June | Youth Day |
| 9 August | National Women's Day |
| 24 September | Heritage Day |
| 16 December | Day of Reconciliation |
| 25 December | Christmas Day |
| 26 December | Day of Goodwill |

**When a holiday falls on Sunday**, the Monday following is a public holiday by operation of the Public Holidays Act s2(2).

## 8. Practical flags for legal clinic matters

The following situations require jurisdiction awareness and should be identified at intake:

| Flag | Issue | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Claim just above R20,000 | Small Claims Court unavailable | Advise Magistrates' Court or negotiate reduction |
| Divorce instruction | Cannot go to Magistrates' Court (District) | High Court only; Legal Aid Board covers basic divorces |
| Will validity dispute | Magistrates' Court has no jurisdiction | High Court required |
| Labour dismissal | CCMA referral first, not court | 30-day referral deadline from dismissal — check urgency |
| State organ as defendant | Government Institutions Protection Act and/or PAJA review | Summons must be served on the State Attorney; 6-month notice under Act 40 of 2002 |
| Urgent interdict | Application to High Court, not Magistrates' Court | Certificate of urgency required; attend duty judge |
| Eviction (PIE) | Separate PIE Act process | Magistrates' Court or High Court depending on parties; PNA order required first |
