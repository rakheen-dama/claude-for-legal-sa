# SA Procedural Rules — South African Framework

This overlay covers South African procedural rules for Magistrates' Courts and the High Court, including time computation, service rules, key deadlines, document formats, and prescription periods. It is loaded by document-drafting, deadline-check, and intake-assessment skills when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Magistrates' Courts Rules — time computation

### 1.1 Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays (Rule 1(2))

Magistrates' Courts Rule 1(2) provides that where the Rules prescribe a period of time for the doing of any act, and that period ends on a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday, the period is extended to the first court day after that date.

**Key principle:** Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays are excluded from counting when the Rules prescribe a period, **unless the contrary appears** from the Rule or context.

Examples:
- A plea is due 20 days after delivery of notice of intention to defend. If day 20 falls on a Saturday, the plea is due on the following Monday (or Tuesday if Monday is a public holiday).
- If any day within a prescribed period is a Sunday or public holiday, that day is skipped.

### 1.2 December–January recess (Rule 21B)

Rule 21B excludes the days between **16 December and 15 January** (both dates inclusive) from the calculation of any period prescribed by the Rules for the delivery of a pleading or document.

**Practical effect:**
- A plea due on 20 December is not due until 16 January (the first day after the recess).
- A 20-day period beginning on 1 December runs: 1–15 December (15 days counted), then resumes on 16 January and runs for 5 more days. The plea would then be due on 20 January.
- Attorneys must build December deadlines into their diary before taking on new matters in November.

**Caution:** Rule 21B applies to **pleadings and documents** under the Rules. It does not automatically extend CCMA referral deadlines or PAJA review deadlines — those are governed by their own legislation.

### 1.3 Counting method

South African procedural rules use **inclusive counting** from the day after the triggering event unless the Rule specifies otherwise:

1. The day on which the act is done or the document is served is **excluded** from the count.
2. The last day is **included** unless it falls on a weekend or public holiday (see Rule 1(2)).
3. Registered post: the posting date is excluded; the deemed service date (4th day after postmarked date) is day zero, with the period beginning on day 1 thereafter.

## 2. Service rules (Magistrates' Courts Rule 9)

### 2.1 Service through the sheriff

All process in Magistrates' Court proceedings must be served by the **sheriff** of the court having jurisdiction in the area where the person to be served is to be found (MCR Rule 9(1)). Parties (or their attorneys) cannot serve process themselves, except as specifically permitted (e.g., agreement to accept service, electronic delivery).

The sheriff is an independent officer of the court appointed under the Sheriff's Act 90 of 1986. The sheriff's return of service is conclusive evidence of the facts stated in it unless set aside by the court.

### 2.2 Service hours (Rule 9(3)(b))

Service of process by the sheriff is restricted to **07:00–19:00** on weekdays. Service after 19:00 or before 07:00 is irregular and may be set aside on application. The sheriff may serve on a Saturday but may not serve on a Sunday or public holiday except as provided in Rule 9(2)(b).

### 2.3 No service on Sundays or public holidays (Rule 9(2)(b))

As a general rule, no service may be effected on a Sunday or public holiday. **Exceptions** permitted by Rule 9(2)(b):
- Interdicts
- Warrants of arrest
- Attachment orders

These may be served on a Sunday or public holiday if the court has specifically authorised it or if the nature of the relief requires immediate service. The return of service must record the basis for Sunday/holiday service.

### 2.4 Deemed service by registered post (Rule 9(10)(c)(i))

Where service by registered post is permitted, a document is deemed to have been served at **10:00 on the fourth day after the day shown on the post office receipt**, excluding Sundays and public holidays.

Example: Letter posted on Wednesday. The fourth business day after Wednesday is Tuesday (Thursday, Friday, Monday, Tuesday). Deemed served at 10:00 on Tuesday.

**Practical note:** Registered post service is slow and carries the risk of non-delivery without recall. For time-sensitive documents, personal service by the sheriff is strongly preferred.

### 2.5 Address for service (Rule 5)

A party who sues or appears in the Magistrates' Court must give an address for service **within 15km of the courthouse** where three or more attorneys practise (Rule 5(1)). In smaller towns where fewer than three attorneys practise, the court may direct an appropriate address. The address for service must be a physical address (not a post office box).

Failure to give a proper address for service may result in the claim or appearance being set aside or costs being awarded against the defaulting party.

### 2.6 Electronic service and delivery

Rule 1's definition of "deliver" includes electronic delivery **by agreement between the parties or by court order**. Without such agreement, process must be served by the sheriff in the traditional manner.

Practical steps for electronic service by agreement:
1. Record the agreement in writing (an email exchange suffices).
2. Specify the email address to which documents will be delivered.
3. Specify the time at which emailed documents will be deemed delivered (e.g., if sent before 16:00, same day; after 16:00, next business day).
4. Keep records of all delivery correspondence.

## 3. Key deadlines — Magistrates' Court

| Step | Deadline | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of intention to defend | As stated in summons (usually 10 court days) | MCR Rule 11 |
| Plea | 20 days after delivery of notice of intention to defend | MCR Rule 12(b) |
| Summary judgment notice | Within 15 days after notice to defend | MCR Rule 14(2) |
| Application notice before hearing | At least 10 days before hearing (20 days if State) | MCR Rule 9(12)(a)–(b) |
| Answering affidavit | 10 days after service of founding affidavit / notice of motion | MCR Rule 55(1)(g)(ii) |
| Replying affidavit | 10 days after service of answering affidavit | MCR Rule 55(1)(g)(iii) |
| Notice of amendment | 10 days notice before effecting amendment | MCR Rule 55A |
| Notice of set-down | 10 days notice to all parties | MCR Rule 55(1)(b) |
| Withdrawal of admission | Before close of pleadings, or by leave of court | MCR Rule 22 |

### 3.1 Notice of intention to defend — key notes

The notice of intention to defend must:
1. State the defendant's address for service (within 15km of the courthouse — Rule 5).
2. Be delivered to the plaintiff's attorney of record.
3. Be filed with the clerk of court.

Failure to deliver timeously results in the plaintiff being entitled to apply for default judgment without further notice.

### 3.2 Summary judgment (Rule 14)

Summary judgment is available where the defendant's defence is unarguable on the pleadings. The plaintiff applies within 15 days of receiving the notice of intention to defend. The defendant may oppose by filing an affidavit disclosing a bona fide defence. Where the defendant discloses a triable issue, summary judgment must be refused.

## 4. Uniform Rules of Court — High Court

### 4.1 Applications (Rule 6)

All applications in the High Court are brought by notice of motion (Rule 6(1)), supported by a founding affidavit deposed to by the applicant or a person with personal knowledge. The notice of motion must:

1. State the relief sought with sufficient particularity.
2. Specify the return date (the date of the hearing).
3. Be supported by the founding affidavit, which must set out all material facts the applicant relies on.

### 4.2 Service of notice of motion (Rule 6(5))

The notice of motion must be served on all respondents at least **5 court days** before the date of the hearing. "Court days" excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays.

For urgent applications, the applicant must obtain a shorter time period from the duty judge and file a certificate of urgency explaining:
- Why the matter is urgent.
- What the applicant stands to lose if not heard urgently.
- Whether the urgency was self-created.

### 4.3 Answering and replying affidavits (Rule 6(5)(d)–(e))

- **Answering affidavit:** The respondent must deliver an answering affidavit within the time specified in the notice of motion or as directed by the court.
- **Replying affidavit:** The applicant may deliver a replying affidavit within the time specified or 10 days after service of the answering affidavit.
- **Rule in Plascon-Evans:** Where there is a dispute of fact on the papers, the court must generally accept the respondent's version unless it is clearly untenable, implausible, or contradicted by undisputed facts.

### 4.4 Interim relief in matrimonial matters (Rule 43)

Rule 43 provides a simplified procedure for **interim relief** in divorce and other matrimonial proceedings pending the final determination of the divorce. A Rule 43 application may seek:

1. Maintenance pendente lite (maintenance pending the finalisation of the divorce).
2. A contribution to legal costs.
3. Interim custody or contact arrangements.
4. Interim use of the matrimonial home or other assets.

Rule 43 applications are decided on affidavit, without oral evidence, by a judge in chambers. The standard is what is reasonable and fair in all the circumstances. There is no appeal against a Rule 43 order — the remedy for an unsatisfactory interim order is an urgent application to vary it or expedited prosecution of the divorce.

### 4.5 Review of administrative decisions (Rule 53; PAJA s7)

Administrative decisions by organs of state are subject to review under the Promotion of Administrative Justice Act 3 of 2000 (PAJA). Key procedural requirements:

1. **180-day rule (PAJA s7(1)):** A review application must be brought within 180 days of the date on which the applicant became aware of the action, or ought reasonably to have become aware of it. Failure to comply with this deadline requires an application for condonation.
2. **Rule 53 procedure:** The applicant delivers a notice of motion and founding affidavit. The respondent must, within 15 days, dispatch the record of the administrative decision to the registrar and the applicant.
3. **Supplementary affidavit:** After receiving the record, the applicant may deliver a supplementary founding affidavit addressing the contents of the record.
4. **Exhaustion of internal remedies (PAJA s7(2)):** A court may not review an administrative action until internal remedies have been exhausted, unless the court, in exceptional circumstances, exempts the applicant.

## 5. Common clinic document formats

### 5.1 Combined summons (Magistrates' Court)

A combined summons combines the summons (the court's command to appear) with the particulars of claim (the factual basis for the claim) in one document. It is the standard pleading for defended civil matters.

Structure:
1. **Header** — court name, case number (allocated on filing), parties.
2. **Command to defendant** — the standard command to appear and deliver notice of intention to defend within 10 court days.
3. **Particulars of claim** — numbered paragraphs setting out:
   - Jurisdiction (why this court has jurisdiction).
   - The defendant's identity and address.
   - The cause of action (contract, delict, enrichment, etc.).
   - The facts giving rise to the claim.
   - The relief claimed.
4. **Prayer** — the specific order sought, including costs.
5. **Signature** — attorney's signature, address for service, date.

### 5.2 Notice of motion (Magistrates' Court — Form 2)

Applications in the Magistrates' Court are brought by notice of motion supported by an affidavit. The notice of motion must:

1. Address the respondent.
2. Specify the return date (date of hearing).
3. State that the respondent must notify the applicant's attorney if the matter is opposed, and deliver an answering affidavit within the time prescribed.
4. Attach and incorporate the founding affidavit by reference.

### 5.3 Founding affidavit — standard structure

All applications are supported by a founding affidavit. The structure is:

1. **Introduction** — deponent's identity, capacity, and personal knowledge.
2. **Background facts** — numbered paragraphs; each paragraph addresses a single fact.
3. **Legal basis** — brief statement of the legal right or rule invoked (e.g., "In terms of the Domestic Violence Act...").
4. **Relief sought** — the specific order the applicant is asking the court to make.
5. **Annexures** — supporting documents annexed and identified by letter (e.g., "Annexure 'A' is a copy of the letter of demand").
6. **Verification** — "I verify that the contents of this affidavit are true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief."
7. **Commissioner of oaths** — signature block for the commissioner.

**Drafting principles:**
- Each paragraph must deal with one subject only.
- Hearsay must be identified as such and the source given.
- Legal argument belongs in heads of argument, not the affidavit.
- The deponent must have personal knowledge of the facts deposed to (or clearly indicate the basis of knowledge).

### 5.4 Protection order application — DVA Form 6 (J480)

An application for a protection order under the Domestic Violence Act 116 of 1998 is brought on Form 6 (J480). The process is:

1. **Complainant completes Form 6** at the Clerk of Court's office (no attorney required).
2. **Clerk issues interim protection order** without hearing from the respondent (ex parte) if there is prima facie evidence of domestic violence and the respondent must be given the opportunity to be heard.
3. **Interim order plus return date** — the interim order is served on the respondent along with the Form 6 and a notice to appear on the return date.
4. **Return date** — the respondent may oppose the grant of the final order. If not opposed, the final order is granted. If opposed, a hearing is held.
5. **Final protection order** — served on the respondent; a copy is given to the South African Police Service to enforce the order.

**Note for clinic practitioners:** Do not discourage clients from making DVA applications. The threshold for an interim order is low (prima facie evidence of domestic violence). If the client faces immediate danger, an urgent police protection order under DVA s8(4) is also available.

### 5.5 Letter of demand — standard structure

A letter of demand is not a court document but is often a prerequisite or prudent first step. Structure:

1. **Addressee** — full name and address of debtor.
2. **Demand** — clear statement of what is owed and the basis of the obligation (contract reference, invoice number, amount).
3. **Legal basis** — brief statement of the legal right (e.g., "In terms of the written agreement concluded between the parties on [date]...").
4. **Deadline to respond or pay** — a reasonable period (typically 7–14 days from the date of the letter).
5. **Consequences of non-response** — "If we do not receive payment by [date], we are instructed to proceed with legal action without further notice to you. You will be held liable for costs of suit."
6. **Contact details** — attorney's name, physical address, email, phone.

For **State organs**, the letter of demand also serves as the formal notice required by the Institution of Legal Proceedings Against Certain Organs of State Act 40 of 2002 s3. The letter must be given within **6 months** of the debt becoming due.

## 6. Prescription and limitation periods

South African prescription is governed primarily by the Prescription Act 68 of 1969. Prescription begins to run when the debt is due (s12(1)) and is interrupted by service of a summons, a written acknowledgement of liability, or a payment (s14–15).

**Extinctive prescription** (s10): A debt is extinguished by prescription — the creditor loses the right to sue. This is a substantive defence (not merely procedural) and may be raised at any stage of proceedings, including for the first time on appeal.

**Delay in service:** In terms of s13(1)(f), prescription is interrupted when a debt is the subject of a claim lodged with a court — but only from the date of service, not the date of filing. Filing a summons that is never served does not interrupt prescription.

### 6.1 Prescription and limitation table

| Claim type | Prescription period | Special notice requirement | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delict / contract (general) | 3 years | — | Prescription Act s11(d) |
| State organ claim (delict or contract) | 3 years | 6-month written notice required before institution of proceedings (Act 40 of 2002 s3) | Act 40 of 2002; Prescription Act s11(d) |
| RAF — owner/driver identified | 3 years to lodge claim; 5 years to issue summons | 120-day moratorium after lodging before summons may be issued | RAF Act 56 of 1996 s23–24 |
| RAF — hit-and-run (owner/driver unidentified) | 2 years to lodge claim; 5 years to issue summons | 120-day moratorium; accident must be reported to police within 2 years | RAF Act s23–24 |
| Negotiable instrument (cheque, promissory note) | 6 years | — | Prescription Act s11(c) |
| Mortgage bond debt | 30 years | — | Prescription Act s11(a) |
| Judgment debt | 30 years | — | Prescription Act s11(a) |
| Joint wrongdoer contribution claim | 12 months from date of judgment | — | Apportionment of Damages Act 34 of 1956 s2(6)(b) |
| COIDA workplace injury compensation | 12 months from date of accident | Accident must be reported to employer within 12 months | COIDA 130 of 1993 s39 |
| Fidelity Fund — attorney theft | 3 months from awareness of theft | Notice of claim to Legal Practice Council required | Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014; LPC Rules |
| CCMA referral — unfair dismissal | 30 days from date of dismissal | Condonation required for late referral | LRA s191(1)(b) |
| CCMA referral — unfair labour practice | 90 days from the act or omission | Condonation required for late referral | LRA s191(1)(b) |

### 6.2 Special notice requirements — common pitfalls

**Act 40 of 2002 (State organs):** The 6-month notice requirement applies to delicts and contractual claims against any organ of state. An "organ of state" is broadly defined and includes municipalities, provincial departments, SAPS, and national government departments. The notice must be in writing and must specify:
- The identity of the person delivering the notice.
- The nature of the debt or claim.
- The facts from which the debt or claim arose.

Failure to give notice is not necessarily fatal — the court may condone non-compliance if it would be unreasonable to decline condonation and the organ of state was not prejudiced (Act 40 of 2002 s3(4)).

**RAF claims:** The distinction between identified and unidentified defendants matters for the prescription period. A claim must be lodged with the RAF on a Form 1 (for injuries) or appropriate form. The lodging of the claim starts the 120-day moratorium clock, during which the RAF must make an offer. Summons may only be issued after the moratorium expires or the RAF rejects the claim.

## 7. Court filing and process administration

### 7.1 Filing at the Magistrates' Court

Documents are filed at the **Clerk of Court's office** (not a registrar — that title is used in the High Court). Practical requirements:
- Original plus at least one copy for the court file and one copy per party.
- Documents must be hole-punched and paginated where they form part of an application book.
- Filing fee must accompany the filing (fee schedule under GN R1444 of 2015, as updated).
- Documents filed after 15:00 are diarised as filed the following court day.

### 7.2 Filing at the High Court

Documents are filed at the **Registrar's office**. Gauteng and Western Cape have implemented e-filing portals. Hard copy filing is still accepted. Urgent applications are filed with the duty registrar and must be accompanied by a certificate of urgency.

### 7.3 Service — proof and returns

The sheriff returns a signed **return of service** (or nulla bona in execution matters) to the attorney. The original is filed with the clerk/registrar; copies are kept on file. Where service was by registered post, the post office receipt and the delivery confirmation (or tracking printout) should be kept on file.

A return of service creates a rebuttable presumption that service was effected as recorded. A defendant who wishes to challenge service must do so by way of affidavit in an application to set aside the default judgment.

## 8. High-risk procedural flags for legal clinic matters

| Flag | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| December deadline approaching | Rule 21B recess may extend deadline into January — or attorney may miss it | Calculate actual deadline with recess; calendar diarised under both actual and recess-extended date |
| State organ as defendant | 6-month notice under Act 40 of 2002 is a prerequisite | Check whether notice was given; calculate gap between notice and institution date |
| RAF claim | Separate lodgment/summons deadlines; moratorium periods | Lodge with RAF first; diarise 120-day moratorium and 3/2-year prescription dates |
| Prescription nearing | Prescription extinguishes the debt — not merely a limitation period | Issue and serve summons before prescription expires; filing without service does not interrupt |
| Emergency protection order | DVA interim order can be obtained ex parte on same day | Client should attend Clerk of Court with Form 6; no attorney required |
| Administrative decision | PAJA 180-day review period; exhaustion of internal remedies | Check decision date; calculate 180 days; confirm internal remedies exhausted |
| CCMA referral | 30-day referral window from dismissal | Check dismissal date immediately at intake; condonation is not guaranteed |
