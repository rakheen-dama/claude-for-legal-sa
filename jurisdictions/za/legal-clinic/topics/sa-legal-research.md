# Legal Research — South African Framework

This overlay is loaded by research-start and memo skills when jurisdiction = ZA. It replaces US legal research methodology and tools (Westlaw, CourtListener, Trellis, and equivalents) with South African equivalents. All research guidance, database references, and citation formats in skill output must follow the standards set out below.

---

## 1. SA Legal Research Databases

### 1.1 SAFLII (Southern African Legal Information Institute)

- **Access:** Free, open access — primary free database (replaces CourtListener)
- **URL:** saflii.org
- **Content:** Case law from all SA courts — Constitutional Court, Supreme Court of Appeal (SCA), all High Court divisions, Labour Court, Labour Appeal Court (LAC), Land Claims Court, Competition Appeal Court, and other specialist courts; some legislation
- **Search:** Full-text keyword search; browse by court and year
- **Citation format:** Neutral citation — *[2020] ZACC 1* (year, court code, sequence number)
- **Limitation:** Not all judgments are reported here; coverage may lag behind commercial databases for recent decisions

### 1.2 Juta / JutaStat

- **Access:** Subscription (university library access)
- **Content:** SA Law Reports (SA), SA Criminal Law Reports (SACR), Industrial Law Journal (ILJ), consolidated legislation, Government Gazette, Lawsa encyclopaedia, Juta commentary series
- **Replaces:** Westlaw (primary commercial research tool in US)
- **Strong for:** Consolidated statutes, annotated commentary, labour law (ILJ)

### 1.3 LexisNexis SA / Lexis+

- **Access:** Subscription (university library access)
- **Content:** All SA Law Reports (All SA), Butterworths Constitutional Law Reports (BCLR), Butterworths Labour Law Reports (BLLR), Butterworths Arbitration Law Reports (BALR), Pensions Law Reports, Competition Law Reports, Judgments Online (JOL), Government Gazette
- **Replaces:** LexisNexis US
- **Strong for:** Labour law reports (BLLR, BALR), constitutional law (BCLR)

### 1.4 Sabinet Legal

- **Access:** Subscription
- **Content:** Consolidated statutes (most current versions of acts in force), Government Gazettes (1910–present), provincial legislation, regulations
- **Strong for:** Finding the current, in-force text of any legislation; tracing amendment history

### 1.5 Government Gazette

- **Access:** Free via opengazettes.org.za; also available on Sabinet and LexisNexis
- **Content:** New legislation, amendments, regulations, notices — official record of all new law
- **Critical for:** Annual threshold updates (BCEA earnings threshold, Small Claims Court limit), newly promulgated regulations, and commencement dates

## 3. SA Citation Format

### 3.1 Law Report Citations

**Format:** *Year (Volume) Reporter Page (Court)*

- *2020 (3) SA 1 (CC)* — 2020, volume 3, South African Law Reports, page 1, Constitutional Court
- *2019 (6) SA 463 (SCA)* — Supreme Court of Appeal
- *2018 (3) BCLR 289 (CC)* — Butterworths Constitutional Law Reports

**SAFLII neutral citations** use the format *[Year] Court-code Sequence*:
- *[2020] ZACC 1* — Constitutional Court
- *[2021] ZASCA 88* — Supreme Court of Appeal
- *[2022] ZALCJHB 100* — Labour Court, Johannesburg

## 5. Legislation Sources

- **Acts of Parliament:** Sabinet Legal (current consolidated versions), Juta, LexisNexis
- **Regulations:** Government Gazette (via Sabinet or opengazettes.org.za)
- **Bills:** Parliamentary Monitoring Group (pmg.org.za), Juta, LexisNexis
- **Provincial legislation:** Provincial Government Gazettes (Sabinet)
- **Practice directives:** SAFLII (under "Rules of Courts"), Juta

When citing legislation, always confirm you are using the current consolidated version — SA statutes are frequently amended without a full reprint being published. Sabinet is the most reliable source for the current in-force text.

---

## 6. Key Secondary Sources

- **Lawsa (Law of South Africa):** Encyclopaedic legal reference published by Juta. The SA equivalent of American Jurisprudence (Am Jur). Organised by subject area; essential starting point for unfamiliar topics.
- **Juta commentary series:** Annotated statutes with integrated case law references — preferred for statutory interpretation work.
- **De Rebus:** Monthly journal of the Law Society of South Africa. Practice-oriented articles; useful for recent procedural and regulatory developments.
- **SA law journals (academic):** Stellenbosch Law Review, South African Law Journal (SALJ), Tydskrif vir Hedendaagse Romeins-Hollandse Reg (THRHR), De Jure, Potchefstroom Electronic Law Journal (PER/PELJ).

## 7. Research Starting Methodology

When the research-start skill produces a research roadmap for an SA matter, follow this sequence:

1. **Start with SAFLII** — free, comprehensive case law. Search by keyword, filter by court and date range. Note neutral citations for any relevant judgments.
2. **Check legislation on Sabinet** — find the current consolidated version of the relevant statute. Note the latest amendment date.
3. **Cross-reference on Juta or LexisNexis** — annotated versions show how courts have interpreted specific sections; check whether key sections have been judicially considered.
4. **Check the Government Gazette** — search opengazettes.org.za or Sabinet for recent amendments, threshold changes, and regulations made under the statute.
5. **Secondary sources** — Lawsa for an encyclopaedic overview of the area of law; journal articles for academic analysis of contested points.

**Prohibited references in SA output:** NEVER reference Westlaw, CourtListener, Trellis, Fastcase, Bloomberg Law, or any other US-specific research tool when producing output for an SA matter.

## 8. Jurisdiction-Specific Flags

- South Africa follows a common law tradition derived from Roman-Dutch law and English law. Where there is no statutory provision, courts apply common law principles — these are not found in a code but in case law and authoritative texts.
- The Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996 is the supreme law. Any statute or conduct inconsistent with it is invalid. Always consider constitutional validity for novel or contested matters.
- Legislation numbering includes the year: *Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995*, not *Labour Relations Act (1995)*. Always cite with act number and year.
- SA courts do not use the North/South/East/West circuit naming used in US federal courts. High Court divisions are named by province and city (see abbreviation table in section 3.2).
