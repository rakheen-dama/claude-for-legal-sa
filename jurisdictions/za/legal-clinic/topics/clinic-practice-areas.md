# Common Law Clinic Practice Areas — South African Framework

This overlay covers the most common practice areas handled by South African university law clinics. It is loaded by client-intake, draft, and memo skills when jurisdiction = ZA. Provides SA-specific templates, statutory frameworks, and procedural requirements for each practice area.

## Family and Domestic Violence

### DVA Protection Orders

Applications for protection orders are governed by the Domestic Violence Act 116 of 1998 (DVA).

**Standing and application:**

- DVA s4: any complainant may apply for a protection order at the Magistrate's Court in the jurisdiction where the complainant or respondent ordinarily resides, or where the domestic violence occurred
- DVA s4(3): application may also be brought on behalf of the complainant by any person who has a material interest in the wellbeing of the complainant (social worker, friend, family member)
- DVA s4(5): **URGENT** — where undue hardship would be caused if the application were made only during court hours, the application may be brought before any magistrate or justice of the peace outside court hours

**Interim protection order:**

- DVA s5(2): the court **must** issue an interim protection order if there is *prima facie* evidence of domestic violence and the complainant would suffer undue hardship if the order is not issued immediately
- Form 6 (J480) is the prescribed application form. Required sections:
  1. Complainant details (full name, address, contact)
  2. Respondent details (full name, last known address, relationship to complainant)
  3. History of abuse (nature, frequency, dates, injuries)
  4. Urgency (why immediate order is needed)
  5. Terms sought (specific prohibitions, exclusion from home, surrender of firearms)
- DVA s5(5): return date must be at least 10 days after service on the respondent; respondent may anticipate the return date on 24 hours' notice
- Service: SAPS must serve the interim order on the respondent within 24 hours of receiving it from the court clerk

**Final protection order:**

- DVA s6(1): at the return date, if the respondent does not appear and proper service has been proved, the court may issue a final order on the basis of *prima facie* evidence
- If the respondent appears, the matter is heard and evidence is tested

**Enforcement:**

- DVA s8: a suspended warrant of arrest is issued together with the protection order; breach of the order allows immediate arrest without further warrant
- Clinic must explain the suspended warrant process to the client and ensure SAPS serves both documents together

**Mandatory reporting:**

- DVA s2B: any person who suspects domestic violence has been committed against a child, person with disabilities, or older person must immediately report this to a social worker or a member of SAPS; failure to report is an offence

### Maintenance

- Maintenance Act 99 of 1998 s2: the duty to maintain exists between parents and their minor children regardless of the marital status of the parents; also applies to stepchildren and persons in loco parentis
- s6: proceedings are initiated by lodging a complaint at the maintenance office of the Magistrate's Court; a maintenance officer investigates, attempts mediation, and may refer to court
- s16: a maintenance order is granted after the court considers the needs of the dependent, the means and earning capacity of the person against whom the order is sought, and the standard of living of the parties
- s31: failure to comply with a maintenance order is a criminal offence; penalty includes a fine or imprisonment up to one year

**Common clinic scenario:** unmarried mother seeking maintenance for a child whose father denies paternity or refuses to pay; clinic assists with:

1. Lodging complaint at maintenance office (no legal representation needed at this stage)
2. Preparing supporting documentation (birth certificate, proof of expenses, proof of father's income if obtainable)
3. Advising on paternity inquiry if contested (Children's Act s26)
4. Attending court on the return date

### Children Act — Parental Rights and Care Proceedings

- Children's Act 38 of 2005 s18: **parental responsibilities and rights** encompass four distinct elements — care, contact, guardianship, and maintenance; a parent may hold all four or only some
- s21: an unmarried father acquires full parental responsibilities and rights if at the time of the child's birth he was living with the mother in a permanent life-partnership, or if he consented to being identified as the father or paid damages in terms of customary law, subsequently lived with the mother, or contributed to upbringing or expenses for a reasonable period
- s45: every Magistrate's Court is a children's court for purposes of the Act; no special designation is required
- s7: the best interests of the child is the paramount consideration in all matters concerning the care, protection, and wellbeing of a child
- s150: a child in need of care and protection is one who has been abandoned, abused, exploited, or is living in circumstances that seriously harm their wellbeing; court may issue an order placing the child in alternative care

**Mandatory reporting — CRITICAL:**

- s110(1): every legal practitioner, social worker, teacher, medical practitioner, and other listed professional who has reasonable grounds to conclude that a child has been abused (including physically, sexually, or through deliberate neglect) **must** report this to a designated child protection organisation (DCPO), a provincial department of social development (DSD) official, or SAPS via Form 22; failure to report is a criminal offence with a maximum penalty of 10 years' imprisonment
- Sexual Offences Act 32 of 2007 s54: **any person** who has knowledge that a sexual offence has been committed against a child **must** report this to a police official immediately; failure is an offence

## Housing and Eviction

### PIE Act Framework

The Prevention of Illegal Eviction and Unlawful Occupation of Land Act 19 of 1998 (PIE Act) governs all residential evictions of persons who occupy land without the consent of the owner.

- PIE Act s4: no person may be evicted from land without a court order
- Court must consider all relevant circumstances, including the rights and needs of the elderly, children, disabled, and particularly vulnerable persons, and whether alternative land or accommodation is available
- If an occupier has been in occupation for more than 6 months, the court must consider whether land or housing has been or can reasonably be made available by an organ of state
- PIE Act s4(2): at least 14 days' written notice must be served on the occupier and the municipality before an eviction application is set down for hearing; notice must be in the official language the occupier understands
- PIE Act s4(7): the court may suspend the eviction order for a period and impose conditions if eviction would be unjust and inequitable

**Clinic role in PIE matters:**

1. Advise occupier of their rights to oppose the eviction
2. Check procedural compliance: notice, service, municipality notice
3. File notice to oppose and opposing affidavit
4. Attend court; raise any procedural defects and substantive grounds
5. If eviction granted, advise on access to temporary emergency accommodation (municipalities have constitutional duty)

### Rental Housing Tribunal

The Rental Housing Act 50 of 1999 establishes provincial Rental Housing Tribunals as an accessible, free alternative to court.

- No legal representation required before the tribunal (but clinics may assist with preparation)
- Unfair practice complaints: maintenance failures, unlawful lease terms, illegal lockouts, deposit disputes, harassment
- Landlord obligations: maintain premises in habitable condition; provide written receipts; deposit must be held in interest-bearing account; return deposit within 14 days of termination less proven damage
- Tenant obligations: pay rent when due; use premises reasonably; not sublet without consent
- Tribunal may issue binding orders; non-compliance may be referred to Magistrate's Court

## Consumer and Debt

### Consumer Protection Act

Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 (CPA) applies to all transactions and promotional offers in the ordinary course of business within South Africa.

- CPA s8–17: consumer rights framework — right to equality in the consumer market, right to privacy, right to choose, right to disclosure and information, right to fair and responsible marketing, right to fair and honest dealing, right to fair, just, and reasonable terms and conditions, right to fair value, good quality, and safety
- CPA s56: implied warranty of quality on all goods sold; goods must be reasonably suitable for the purpose generally intended, be of good quality and free of defects, and comply with any applicable standards
- CPA s48–52: unfair contract terms — terms that are unfair, unreasonable, or unjust may be severed or set aside by a court or Tribunal
- National Consumer Commission (NCC): complaints may be lodged online or at regional offices; NCC investigates and may refer matters to the Consumer Tribunal
- National Consumer Tribunal: adjudicates complaints referred by NCC or directly by consumers

---

### National Credit Act

National Credit Act 34 of 2005 (NCA) governs all credit agreements in South Africa.

- NCA s86: a consumer who is, or is likely to become, over-indebted may apply to a debt counsellor for debt review; the debt counsellor assesses the consumer's financial position and negotiates a rearrangement with credit providers
- NCA s79–83 (reckless credit): a credit agreement is reckless if the credit provider failed to conduct proper affordability assessment, or granted credit knowing (or ought to have known) the consumer did not understand the risks; court may set aside a reckless credit agreement
- NCA s103(5): the **in duplum rule** — interest, fees, and charges in respect of a credit agreement may not exceed the unpaid principal debt; once interest equals the outstanding principal, it stops running until the consumer makes a payment
- Debt collectors: regulated under the Debt Collectors Act 114 of 1998; harassment, threats, and false representations are prohibited; complaints to the Council for Debt Collectors

**Common clinic scenario:** client with multiple credit agreements (vehicle finance, clothing accounts, payday loans) who is being contacted by multiple debt collectors simultaneously; clinic assists with:

1. Full credit disclosure: obtain free credit report from credit bureau
2. Assess whether over-indebted and qualify for debt review (NCA s79)
3. Refer to registered debt counsellor (NCR-registered) if appropriate
4. Advise on rights against abusive debt collection practices
5. If reckless credit suspected: prepare affidavit and apply to Magistrate's Court or National Consumer Tribunal

## Criminal and Delict

### Criminal Matters

- CPA s60: a bail application may be brought at any stage of proceedings, before a Magistrate's Court or High Court
- **Bail categories:**

| Schedule | Bail authority | Onus |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule 1 (minor offences) | Any court | State to show why bail refused |
| Schedule 5 (serious offences e.g., GBH, robbery) | Magistrate or judge | Accused must show exceptional circumstances / interests of justice |
| Schedule 6 (very serious e.g., murder, rape, treason) | Magistrate or judge | Accused must show exceptional circumstances why interests of justice permit release |

- CPA s112: plea procedure — if an accused pleads guilty, the court questions the accused to ensure the plea is made freely and voluntarily, that the accused understands the charge, and that the accused admits all elements of the offence; the court may reject the guilty plea if not satisfied
- Constitution s35(3)(f): every accused has the right to have a legal practitioner assigned at state expense if substantial injustice would otherwise result; application must be made to the Director of Legal Aid (Legal Aid SA)
- Legal Aid SA referral: if the matter falls within Legal Aid SA mandate (see clinic-client-access.md), refer immediately; do not act without resources to run the matter through to conclusion
- CPA s309: appeal from Magistrate's Court to High Court on conviction or sentence or both; notice of appeal must be filed within 14 days of conviction/sentence (court has discretion to condone late filing)

### Delict — Personal Injury and Damages

Common law clinic delict matters include motor vehicle accidents, assault by SAPS members, medical negligence, and occupier's liability injuries.

**Prescription:**

- Prescription Act 68 of 1969 s11(d): the general prescription period is **3 years** from the date the debt became due; a debt for unliquidated damages becomes due when the creditor has knowledge of the identity of the debtor and the facts giving rise to the debt (s12(3))
- Special periods: RAF claims — 3 years from date of accident to lodge claim (2 years for hit-and-run); COIDA claims — 12 months from date of accident to lodge with Compensation Fund

**Notice requirements for state organs — CRITICAL:**

- Institution of Legal Proceedings Against Certain Organs of State Act 40 of 2002 s3: before a summons may be issued against an **organ of state**, written notice of the intention to institute proceedings must be delivered to the organ within **6 months** of the cause of action arising; failure to give notice bars the claim unless the court condones the non-compliance (s3(4), only where debt not prescribed and good cause shown)
- Organs of state affected: SAPS (police brutality / wrongful arrest), provincial and national government hospitals (medical negligence), municipalities (negligent road maintenance, service delivery failures)
- Clinic must check: Is the opposing party a State organ? Has the 6-month period passed? If close to expiry, serve notice immediately even if claim details are not fully quantified

**Road Accident Fund (RAF) claims:**

- RAF Act 56 of 1996 s23: lodgement within 3 years of accident (2 years for hit-and-run or where driver unknown)
- Summons must be issued within 5 years of accident or 2 years of lodgement (whichever is later)
- 120-day moratorium: after lodgement, a claimant must allow 120 days for the RAF to investigate before issuing summons (absent acceleration application)
- Forms: RAF1 (accident report), RAF4 (serious injury assessment by medical practitioner)

**Letter of demand — standard elements:**

1. Client details and legal representative details
2. Statement of facts: date, place, circumstances of the incident
3. Legal basis: statutory or common law basis for liability
4. Damages: general and special damages, heads itemised
5. Quantum: amount demanded or reservation of right to quantify
6. Deadline: typically 14 calendar days from date of letter
7. Consequences of non-response: proceedings to follow

## Refugee and Migration

### Refugees Act Framework

Refugees Act 130 of 1998 governs the right to asylum and the status of refugees in South Africa.

**Asylum application process:**

1. Person enters South Africa (lawfully or unlawfully) and wishes to apply for asylum
2. Must present to a Refugee Reception Office (RRO) as soon as practicable — the Standing Committee for Refugee Affairs (SCRA) and Refugee Appeals Authority (now Refugee Appeal Board (RAB)) operate independently
3. DVR (Department of Home Affairs): asylum seeker is interviewed by a Refugee Status Determination Officer (RSDO)
4. Refugees Act s22: an **asylum seeker permit** (Section 22 permit) is issued pending determination; the permit authorises the holder to remain in South Africa and to seek employment
5. s24: if the application is rejected, the applicant may appeal to the Refugee Appeal Board within 30 days of the decision
6. If appeal fails, judicial review under the Promotion of Administrative Justice Act 3 of 2000 (PAJA) in the High Court is available

**Common clinic matters:**

- Unlawful rejection of asylum application without proper consideration of the Convention grounds (well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership of a particular social group)
- Failure to process or unreasonable delay by DHA
- Documentation loss or expiry — Section 22 permit not renewed, resulting in unlawful presence and risk of deportation
- Detention and deportation proceedings

**PAJA review — key grounds (s6):**

- Procedural unfairness (no right to be heard)
- Failure to apply the correct legal standard
- Decision so unreasonable that no reasonable decision-maker could have reached it (Bato Star standard)

**WITS Law Clinic Refugee Unit:** operates as a specialist model; other clinics should consider whether to refer complex refugee matters to specialist units rather than managing in-house without refugee law expertise.

## High-Risk Flags — Law Clinic Practice

The following flags must be checked at every client intake and at each supervision session. Red flags require immediate action.

| # | Flag | Level | Why high-risk | Check | Statute |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prescription approaching | 🔴 | Claim extinguished if missed. Most common malpractice ground in clinic context. | 3yr default; special periods for RAF (3/2yr lodge), COIDA (12 months). Flag at 6/3/1 months before expiry. | Prescription Act s11–12 |
| 2 | State organ notice period | 🔴 | 6-month written notice required before issuing summons against State organs. Failure bars the claim. | Is opposing party SAPS, government hospital, or municipality? Has notice been served? | Act 40 of 2002 s3 |
| 3 | DVA urgency — client safety | 🔴 | DVA s4(5) allows urgent applications outside court hours. Delay endangers client and potentially constitutes negligence. | Immediate physical safety risk? File same-day; brief clinic director. | DVA ss4–5 |
| 4 | Mandatory reporting — child abuse | 🔴 | Legal practitioners are mandatory reporters. Failure to report is a criminal offence with maximum 10 years' imprisonment. | Physical injury, sexual abuse, or deliberate neglect of a child? Report via Form 22 to DCPO/DSD/SAPS immediately. | Children's Act s110(1) |
| 5 | Mandatory reporting — DVA | 🔴 | DVA s2B requires reporting DV against children, elderly persons, and persons with disabilities. | DV against child, older person, or person with disability? Report required regardless of client consent. | DVA s2B |
| 6 | Supervision ratio breach | 🟠 | LPC Rule 37 caps the number of candidate attorneys per supervisor at 6. Exceeding this cap exposes the supervisor and clinic to disciplinary sanction. | Current candidate count per supervisor. Flag at 5 candidates; block new intake at 7. | LPC Rule 37 |
| 7 | Conflict of interest | 🟠 | Clinics serve concentrated communities; opposing parties may both approach the same clinic. Failure to screen results in LPC complaint or professional sanction. | Screen names and entities against case register at intake before creating a file. | LPC Code 3.3–3.4 |
| 8 | Semester handoff — urgent matter | 🟠 | Active matters with approaching deadlines can fall through during end-of-semester candidate rotations. | Audit all active files for court dates, prescription dates, and statutory deadlines within 60 days of handoff. Brief incoming candidate personally. | — |
| 9 | RAF procedural trap | 🟠 | RAF Act imposes overlapping time limits (3yr lodge, 5yr summons, 120-day moratorium) that differ from general prescription rules. Error results in barred claim with no remedy. | Is this a motor vehicle accident claim? Apply RAF Act timelines in addition to Prescription Act. Check hit-and-run (2yr) vs normal (3yr). | RAF Act s23–24 |
| 10 | Small Claims representation bar | 🟡 | No legal representation is permitted in the Small Claims Court. Clinic may advise and assist with documents but cannot appear. | Is the matter a Small Claims matter (R20,000 limit)? Limit clinic role to advice and document preparation only. | Small Claims Court Act s7 |
| 11 | Client communication gap | 🟡 | Failure to keep clients informed is the most common source of LPC complaints against law clinics and practitioners generally. | Has each active client received a written update within the last 30 days? | LPC Code 3.11 |
| 12 | Unauthorised practice | 🟡 | A candidate attorney giving independent legal advice or signing documents without supervision constitutes unauthorised practice. All client-facing output must pass through a supervision gate. | Is any output (letter, advice note, pleading, affidavit) going to a client without supervisor sign-off? | LPA s34(8)(b)(i) |
| 13 | POPIA — client data | 🟡 | Clinics collect sensitive personal information including health data, financial records, and information about crimes. POPIA requires explicit consent for special personal information. | Is intake limited to necessary information only? Is data stored securely? Does the consent form cover all categories collected? | POPIA ss9–12, 26–27 |
| 14 | Court date clash | 🟡 | A candidate attorney with two matters listed in different courts on the same date must escalate immediately. Default judgment or contempt risk is high. | Cross-reference court dates per candidate every week. Flag any overlap within 7 days. Brief supervisor and arrange cover counsel. | — |
