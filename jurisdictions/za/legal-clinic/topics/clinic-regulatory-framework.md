# Law Clinic Regulatory Framework — South African Overlay

This overlay covers the statutory and regulatory framework governing South African law clinics under the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 (LPA) and the Legal Practice Council Rules (LPC Rules). It is loaded by clinic-intake, supervision-check, candidate-management, and compliance-audit skills when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. LPA s34(8) — Law Clinic Definition and Requirements

Section 34(8) of the LPA creates the legal basis for law clinics in South Africa. A body qualifies as a law clinic only if it meets all four cumulative requirements:

1. **University attachment** — the clinic must be attached to a university faculty of law, or to an institution offering equivalent legal education recognised by the LPC (LPA s34(8)(a)).
2. **Supervised service delivery** — legal services must be rendered by or under the supervision of admitted attorneys on the professional staff of the clinic (LPA s34(8)(b)(i)). Supervision is not merely nominal — the supervising attorney is personally accountable for work product quality.
3. **Public accessibility** — the clinic must be accessible to members of the public, ensuring that the target population (indigent or otherwise underserved) can actually obtain its services (LPA s34(8)(a)).
4. **Free-of-charge services** — the clinic must provide services free of charge. The only permitted recovery is disbursements actually incurred (LPA s34(8)(c)).

### 1.1 Prohibition on fee sharing

LPA s34(8)(b)(ii) expressly prohibits any sharing or division of professional fees between the clinic and any third party. This provision:

- Applies even where a matter results in a costs order or settlement that includes an attorney and own-client costs component.
- Does not prevent recovery of disbursements (filing fees, sheriff's fees, counsel fees actually incurred) from a losing party or from a legal aid grant.
- Means the clinic may not operate a referral-fee arrangement with any external practitioner or firm.

### 1.2 Consequence of non-compliance

A body that fails to meet the s34(8) requirements is not a recognised law clinic for LPC purposes and its candidate legal practitioners are not undergoing valid practical vocational training. The university's accreditation status and the candidates' PVTC registration may be affected.

## 2. LPC Rules Part IX — Law Clinics (Rules 36–37)

Part IX of the LPC Rules operationalises LPA s34(8). Rules 36 and 37 set out the recognition criteria and the engagement framework for candidate legal practitioners.

### 2.1 Rule 36 — Annual LPC Recognition

Rule 36.1 requires every law clinic to apply annually to the LPC for recognition. Failure to obtain recognition means the clinic is not authorised to engage candidate legal practitioners under the PVTC framework. The annual recognition application must demonstrate:

| Criterion | Rule reference | What the LPC checks |
|---|---|---|
| Compliance with LPA s34(8)(a) | Rule 36.1(a) | University attachment, public accessibility, free services |
| Proper constitution | Rule 36.1(b) | Formal instrument establishing the clinic (deed of establishment or equivalent) |
| Proper organisation | Rule 36.1(c) | Governance structure, reporting lines, management arrangements |
| Adequate control | Rule 36.1(d) | Supervision hierarchy, file control, quality assurance procedures |

### 2.2 Rule 37 — Engagement of Candidate Legal Practitioners

Rule 37 sets minimum standards for any recognised law clinic that engages candidate legal practitioners under a PVTC. Non-compliance is a disciplinary matter for the supervising attorney and may void the candidate's training period.

#### Rule 37 Requirements Summary

| Rule | Requirement | Practical implication |
|---|---|---|
| 37.1.1 | Direct personal supervision by admitted attorney | Supervisor must be physically available, not merely nominally listed |
| 37.1.2 | Open for business minimum 11 months per year | Clinic must not close for periods that would prejudice PVTC completion |
| 37.1.3 | Proper office systems — telephones, IT, files, diary, library | Adequate infrastructure for professional legal work |
| 37.1.4 | Proper accounting system | Trust accounting compliant with LPC Rules; separate from university general accounts |
| 37.1.5 | Reasonably wide range of work | Candidates must be exposed to varied matters to develop competence |
| 37 Proviso | Maximum 6 candidates per supervisor | Hard cap; breach constitutes disciplinary exposure for the supervising attorney |

#### Rule 37.1.1 — Direct Personal Supervision

"Direct personal supervision" in Rule 37.1.1 is the most frequently misunderstood requirement. It means:

1. The supervising attorney must be a member of the clinic's professional staff (not a practitioner at another firm who "supervises" by correspondence).
2. The supervisor must be available during working hours to review work, answer questions, and approve client-facing output.
3. Remote supervision (email/video only) is not regarded as sufficient for day-to-day PVTC supervision — the supervisor must be accessible in person with reasonable regularity.
4. A supervisor who is away for extended periods must arrange a substitute admitted attorney supervisor on the clinic's professional staff.

#### Rule 37.1.5 — Range of Work

Candidates are entitled to exposure to a "reasonably wide range" of legal work. This means:

- The clinic must not confine candidates exclusively to a single narrow area (e.g., only debt collection matters).
- Candidates should receive exposure to both litigious and non-litigious matters where the clinic's practice area permits.
- A clinic specialising in a particular field (family law, housing law) satisfies the requirement if it offers varied work within that field.

## 3. Candidate Legal Practitioner Framework

### 3.1 Terminology

South African law uses specific statutory terms that differ from informal usage:

| Term | Meaning | LPA reference |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate legal practitioner | A person undergoing practical vocational training (PVT) — either a candidate attorney or a pupil | LPA s1 (definitions) |
| Candidate attorney | CLP undergoing training under an attorney principal | LPA s26 |
| Pupil | CLP undergoing training under an advocate principal | LPA s27 |
| Admitted attorney | A legal practitioner admitted and enrolled on the Roll of Attorneys (distinct from an advocate) | LPA s24 |
| Legal practitioner | Generic term covering both attorneys and advocates | LPA s1 |

Do not use the terms "law student", "student attorney", "articled clerk", or "article clerk" in client-facing documents or correspondence — these terms are either informal or refer to the pre-LPA regime and may create confusion about the candidate's authorisation to practise.

### 3.2 Practical Vocational Training Contract (PVTC)

The PVTC is the formal agreement between the candidate and the principal (the clinic, acting through its supervising attorney). Key parameters:

1. **Duration** — 24 months for candidates without an approved practical legal training course. Reduced to **12 months** for candidates who have completed an approved course of at least 400 contact hours (LPA s26(1)(c)).
2. **LPC enrolment** — the PVTC must be registered with the LPC before training commences. An unregistered PVTC does not count towards the training requirement.
3. **Competency examination** — the candidate must pass the LPC-administered competency-based examination before admission (LPA s26(1)(d)). The clinic is expected to prepare candidates adequately for this examination as part of the training programme.
4. **Interruptions** — extended leave, illness, or clinic closure may affect the validity of the training period. Breaks of more than 30 days ordinarily require LPC notification.

### 3.3 Admission Requirements for Candidate Attorneys

Before the LPC will recommend admission, a candidate attorney must demonstrate (LPA s26(1)):

1. A recognised LLB or equivalent degree.
2. Completion of a registered PVTC with an LPC-recognised principal.
3. Passage of the LPC competency-based examination.
4. Fit and proper status (no pending disciplinary proceedings, no relevant criminal convictions).

## 4. Community Service Obligations

The LPA introduced mandatory community service for legal practitioners and candidates, effective from 1 January 2024.

### 4.1 Annual Requirements

| Category | Annual obligation | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Practising legal practitioners | 40 hours per calendar year | LPA s29(1) |
| Candidate legal practitioners | 8 hours per calendar year | LPA s29(1) read with LPC Guidelines |
| Full-time law clinic staff | Exempt from separate obligation (service rendered counts) | LPC Guidelines reg 8.2.1 |

### 4.2 Supervision During Candidate Community Service

Candidate legal practitioners must be supervised during community service in the same way as during ordinary clinic work. A candidate may not count hours worked independently (without supervisory oversight) towards the community service obligation.

### 4.3 Approved Structures for Community Service (LPA s29(2))

Community service must be rendered through or to one of the following structures:

| Structure type | Examples |
|---|---|
| State service | Department of Justice and Constitutional Development; Legal Aid SA; State Attorney |
| South African Human Rights Commission (SAHRC) | SAHRC advice offices and public education programmes |
| Judicial officer service | Serving as a court preparation officer, registrar's assistant, or equivalent |
| Legal education or training | Presenting legal literacy workshops, community law education programmes |
| Other approved service | Any structure approved by the LPC under s29(2)(e) |

### 4.4 Record-Keeping

The clinic must maintain records of community service hours rendered by candidates and supervisors. These records must be submitted to the LPC on request and may be audited during the annual recognition review.

## 5. LPC Code of Conduct — Key Provisions

The LPC Code of Conduct applies to all practising legal practitioners, including attorneys employed at law clinics. The following provisions are most frequently relevant in a clinic context.

### 5.1 Competence and Timeliness (Clause 3.11)

Clause 3.11 requires every legal practitioner to perform work with the required level of competence and within a reasonable time. This is the most common ground for disciplinary proceedings against clinic attorneys. Clinic-specific implications:

- Heavy caseloads do not excuse delay or inadequate work — the practitioner remains personally responsible.
- Where the clinic's resources are insufficient to handle a matter competently, the matter should be referred rather than retained.
- Candidates may do the preparatory work, but the supervising attorney remains responsible for the quality and timeliness of the output.

### 5.2 Conflict of Interest (Clauses 3.3–3.4)

The conflict of interest rules apply fully in the clinic context, including:

1. **Clause 3.3** — a practitioner may not act for two parties whose interests conflict, even in a clinic context where capacity is strained.
2. **Clause 3.4** — a practitioner who previously acted for a party in a matter may not subsequently act against that party in the same or a related matter.
3. **Clinic-specific risk**: clinics sometimes represent multiple members of the same household or community. Each client must be separately assessed for conflict.

### 5.3 Duty Not to Bring the Profession into Disrepute (Clause 3.15)

Clause 3.15 applies to conduct both inside and outside the clinic. Candidates are not yet legal practitioners for clause 3.15 purposes, but their conduct reflects on the supervising attorney and the clinic. The clinic should have an internal conduct policy that mirrors the Code's standards.

### 5.4 Duty to Report Dishonest Conduct re Trust Money (Rule 54.36)

LPC Rule 54.36 imposes a positive duty on every legal practitioner to report to the LPC any conduct by another practitioner that involves dishonesty in relation to trust money. This is a mandatory obligation — it is not subject to the practitioner's discretion about whether to report.

### 5.5 Reporting Unprofessional Conduct (Clause 5)

Clause 5 encourages (but does not strictly require) practitioners to report unprofessional conduct by peers to the LPC. In a clinic context, this applies to:

- Conduct by supervising attorneys that prejudices candidates.
- Conduct by candidates that, if unaddressed, could harm clients.
- Conduct by external practitioners encountered in the course of clinic work (e.g., on the other side of a matter).

## 6. High-Risk Compliance Flags — Regulatory Framework

| # | Flag | Why it's high-risk | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual LPC recognition not obtained | Clinic is not authorised to engage candidates; PVTC invalid; university accreditation at risk | Confirm current-year LPC recognition certificate is on file (Rule 36.1) |
| 2 | Fee recovery beyond disbursements | Breach of LPA s34(8)(c); potential fee-sharing violation | Review all cost recovery arrangements; confirm only disbursements are recovered |
| 3 | No university faculty attachment | Clinic does not qualify as a law clinic under LPA s34(8)(a) | Confirm formal attachment instrument is current and in writing |
| 4 | PVTC not registered with LPC | Training period does not count; candidate admission may be refused | Check LPC registration confirmation for each active candidate |
| 5 | Clinic closed more than 1 month | Breach of Rule 37.1.2; may void training period for affected candidates | Review clinic calendar; notify LPC if closure exceeds 30 days |
| 6 | Supervision ratio breach | Maximum 6 candidates per supervisor under Rule 37 proviso | Count active candidates per supervisor; flag at 5, hard stop at 7 |
| 7 | Trust account non-compliance | Breach of Rule 37.1.4 and LPC accounting rules; potential disciplinary action | Confirm trust accounting is separate from university operating accounts and subject to LPC audit |
| 8 | Insufficient work range for candidates | Breach of Rule 37.1.5; may prejudice candidate admission | Review matter register; confirm candidates are receiving varied work exposure |
| 9 | Community service hours not recorded | Non-compliance with LPA s29; LPC audit risk | Confirm hours log is maintained and up to date |
| 10 | Conflict of interest not assessed | Breach of LPC Code clauses 3.3–3.4; potential professional misconduct | Confirm conflict check is run for each new matter |
