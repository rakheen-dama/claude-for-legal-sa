# Patents — South African Framework

This overlay covers SA patentability requirements, absolute novelty, statutory exclusions, software patents, infringement, compulsory licences, damages, and the depository examination system. Loaded by invention-intake, fto-triage, and infringement-triage when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Patentability (Patents Act s25)

An invention is patentable in South Africa if it meets three cumulative requirements (Patents Act 57 of 1978, s25(1)):

| Requirement | Description |
|---|---|
| **Novelty** | The invention must be new — not forming part of the state of the art (prior art). SA applies **absolute worldwide novelty**. |
| **Inventive step** | The invention must not be obvious to a person skilled in the relevant art at the priority date. |
| **Industrial applicability** | The invention must be capable of being used or applied in trade, industry, or agriculture. |

---

## 2. Absolute novelty — the critical difference from US practice

**SA requires absolute worldwide novelty. There is NO general 12-month grace period.**

Any prior disclosure anywhere in the world before the priority date constitutes prior art. This includes:

- Conference presentations or poster sessions
- Academic publications, preprints, or technical reports
- Website announcements or product launch pages
- Investor decks shared outside strict NDA
- Product demonstrations or samples provided to third parties
- Social media posts describing the invention
- Patent applications filed by others

**Limited exceptions exist but should not be relied upon:**
- Disclosures at officially recognised international exhibitions (narrow, rarely applicable)
- Unlawful third-party disclosure (must prove the disclosure was made without authority)

**Operational rule:** Counsel inventors to file before any public disclosure. Once a disclosure has been made, obtain a written opinion on novelty impact before advising the client that SA patent rights are available.

**Contrast with US:** 35 USC §102(a)(1) provides a 1-year grace period for the inventor's own disclosures. SA law has no equivalent. Do not advise SA applicants based on US grace period logic.

---

## 3. Excluded subject matter — "as such" qualification (Patents Act s25(2)-(3))

The following are not deemed to be "inventions" and are excluded from patentability (s25(2)):

- Discoveries, scientific theories, mathematical methods
- Literary, dramatic, musical, or artistic works (protected by copyright)
- Schemes, rules, or methods for performing a mental act, playing a game, or doing business
- **Computer programs**
- Presentation of information

**Critical qualification — s25(3):** The exclusions apply **only to the extent that the application relates to that thing as such.** A claim directed to a technical implementation — where the computer program or method has a **technical character** or produces a **technical effect** — can still be patentable.

### Software and business method patents in SA

SA follows a **European-style technical effect test**:

- Claims must emphasise **technical features** and the **technical effect** of the invention
- A pure business method implemented in software with no technical effect is excluded
- A computer-implemented invention that solves a **technical problem** in a **technical way** can be patentable
- Drafting practice: articulate the technical problem the invention solves; claim the technical features rather than the abstract method; avoid purely result-oriented claim language

---

## 4. Other notable exclusions

| Exclusion | Statutory basis | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Methods of treatment of the human or animal body by surgery, therapy, or diagnosis | s25(4)(a) | Excluded from patentability. **Products** (e.g., pharmaceutical compounds, medical devices, diagnostics) for use in such methods CAN be patented. |
| Plant or animal varieties (essentially biological processes) | s25(4)(b) | Excluded |
| Perpetual motion machines (no useful purpose, contrary to physical laws) | s25(4)(c) | Excluded |

## 5. Patent term and annuities

| Milestone | Rule |
|---|---|
| Patent term | 20 years from the filing date (s45) |
| First annuity | Due on the 3rd anniversary of the filing date |
| Subsequent annuities | Due annually (year 4 through year 20) |
| Grace period | A short grace period with surcharge applies for late payment |
| Lapse | Failure to pay annuities results in lapse of the patent |

**Practice note:** Diarise annuity dates carefully. Lapsed patents cannot be restored without a formal application and showing of unintentional delay.

---

## 6. Depository examination system — validity is NOT confirmed by grant

**SA operates a depository (non-examining) patent system.** Patent applications undergo **formal examination only** — CIPC checks completeness, correct form, drawings, and classification. CIPC does **not** conduct substantive examination for novelty, inventive step, or industrial applicability.

**Consequences:**

- A granted SA patent may be invalid on substantive grounds
- Registration at CIPC does **not** equal validity
- Patent validity is tested in **litigation** or **revocation proceedings** before the Commissioner of Patents
- In transactions (IP due diligence, licensing, M&A), assess the validity risk of any SA patent being relied upon — a prior-art search is essential

**Commissioner of Patents:** A Judge of the High Court sits as Commissioner of Patents to hear revocation proceedings, compulsory licence applications, and declarations of non-infringement (s8 of the Patents Act). Patent matters are not heard by CIPC itself.

## 7. Infringement (Patents Act s65-66)

### Product patents

Infringement occurs where any person, without authority, makes, uses, exercises, disposes of, offers to dispose of, imports, or stocks the patented product in South Africa.

### Process patents

Infringement occurs where any person, without authority, uses the patented process; and dealing in a product obtained directly by the patented process constitutes infringement of the process patent.

### Claim construction

SA uses **purposive claim construction** — the court gives the claims their purposive and natural meaning in the context of the specification and the common general knowledge in the art. There is no Markman hearing procedure. Claim interpretation is determined at trial in the context of the specific infringement dispute.

### What is not in SA patent law

- No developed doctrine of **indirect/contributory infringement** equivalent to 35 USC §271(b)-(c)
- No doctrine of **equivalents** as a separate legal rule (though purposive construction achieves similar results in appropriate cases)
- No **enhanced/treble damages** for wilful infringement

## 8. Compulsory licences (Patents Act s56)

The Commissioner of Patents may grant a compulsory licence on application by any person. Grounds include:

| Ground | Description |
|---|---|
| Failure to work | The patentee has failed to work the invention in SA on a reasonable scale to meet demand in SA on reasonable terms |
| Anti-competitive refusal | The patentee refuses to grant licences on reasonable terms, which prejudicially affects the establishment or development of an industry in SA |
| Dependent patent | A patent cannot be worked without infringing an earlier patent, and the second patent represents an important advance |
| Public health | Consistent with TRIPS flexibilities (Doha Declaration) — SA has implemented provisions allowing compulsory licences for pharmaceutical patents in the public health interest |

**Risk assessment:** In pharma and essential technology sectors, compulsory licence risk is real in SA. The mere granting of a SA patent does not guarantee exclusivity if working obligations are not met. Patent holders should maintain a credible working/supply plan.

---

## 9. Civil remedies (Patents Act s67)

| Remedy | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Interdict** | Primary remedy — prevents continued infringement. Interim and final interdicts available. |
| **Damages** | Compensatory only. No treble or enhanced damages available. Calculation: lost profits, price erosion, or reasonable royalty. |
| **Reasonable royalty** | In lieu of or in addition to proven damages — the notional licence fee a willing licensor and willing licensee would have agreed at the time infringement began. |
| **Delivery up** | Infringing articles or implements used in infringement may be delivered up or destroyed. |

---

## 10. Declaration of non-infringement (Patents Act s68)

Any person may apply to the Commissioner for a **declaration that a process or article does not infringe a patent**. Prerequisite: the applicant must first request — and be refused or not receive within a reasonable time — a written acknowledgement of non-infringement from the patentee.

This is a useful freedom-to-operate mechanism distinct from litigation. It places the burden on the patentee to defend the patent's scope.

---

## 11. What NOT to replicate from US practice

| US concept | Why it does not apply in SA |
|---|---|
| **1-year grace period (35 USC §102(a)(1))** | SA has no general grace period. Any prior disclosure before the priority date destroys novelty. Do not advise SA applicants that they have 12 months after disclosure to file. |
| **35 USC §101 / Alice/Mayo** | US subject-matter eligibility analysis for abstract ideas, natural phenomena, laws of nature. SA uses s25(2)-(3) with the European "as such" qualification — a different doctrinal framework. |
| **Markman hearing** | US procedural mechanism for claim construction before a judge. SA has no equivalent — claim interpretation is part of the trial. |
| **Treble damages for wilful infringement (35 USC §284)** | Not available in SA. Damages are compensatory only. |
| **Doctrine of equivalents (Graver Tank)** | A distinct US doctrine. SA uses purposive claim construction — there is no separate doctrine of equivalents. |
| **Hatch-Waxman Act / ANDA process** | US pharma patent linkage regime. SA has no equivalent — separate regulatory and IP systems. |
| **Inter partes review (IPR) / post-grant review (PGR)** | USPTO administrative patent challenge proceedings. SA uses revocation proceedings before the Commissioner of Patents (judicial, not administrative). |
| **Provisional applications and 12-month USPTO priority window as a disclosure strategy** | A SA provisional application (patent application with a provisional specification) buys time for 12 months to file the complete specification, but it does NOT protect against prior art from other sources during that window. It also does not provide the US-style "safe harbour" against your own disclosures. |
