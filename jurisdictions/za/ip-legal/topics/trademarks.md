# Trademarks — South African Framework

This overlay covers SA trademark registration, the confusion test, clearance beyond the register, well-known marks, passing off, defences, and first-to-file rules. Loaded by clearance, infringement-triage, and cease-desist when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Registration and first-to-file

South Africa operates a **first-to-file** trademark system. Priority in a class is determined by filing date, not by date of first use in trade. The Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993 (TMA) governs registration, infringement, and defences.

**Registrar:** Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), Trade Marks Directorate.

**Registrability (TMA s9):** A mark is registrable if it is capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of another in the course of trade. Marks may be inherently distinctive or may have acquired distinctiveness through use.

**Unregistrable marks (TMA s10):** Grounds for refusal include marks that are devoid of distinctive character (s10(1)), descriptive or generic marks (s10(2)), marks without a bona fide claim to proprietorship (s10(3)), marks confusingly similar to earlier marks (s10(12)), marks contrary to law or morality (s10(10)), and deceptive marks (s10(11)).

## 2. CIPC examination process

| Stage | Description | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Filing | File application at CIPC with mark, class(es), goods/services list, applicant details | Day 0 |
| Formalities | CIPC checks completeness, correct form, class list | 1–3 months |
| Substantive examination | Examiner assesses registrability (absolute and relative grounds) | 4–8 months |
| Acceptance or objection | Examiner accepts or issues an office action | 8–12 months |
| Advertisement | Accepted mark advertised in the Patent Journal for opposition (3-month window) | After acceptance |
| Registration | Certificate issued if no opposition (or opposition resolved) | Total: typically 18–24 months |

**Nice Classification:** SA uses the NICE International Classification of Goods and Services (current edition). Multi-class applications are filed per class with a separate fee per class.

**Backlog note:** CIPC examination timelines are subject to significant backlog. Allow 10–18 months for a routine examination outcome; complex matters take longer.

## 3. Confusion test — global appreciation (TMA s34)

SA courts apply a **global appreciation** test for likelihood of confusion. This is NOT the US codified multi-factor approach (du Pont, Polaroid, Sleekcraft).

### What the SA test requires

The court considers:

1. **Visual, aural, and conceptual similarity** of the marks as wholes — marks are compared in their entirety, not dissected feature by feature
2. **Similarity of goods and services** covered by each mark
3. **The average consumer** — a notional, reasonably attentive consumer with imperfect recollection who holds a general impression rather than a precise memory of the earlier mark
4. **Distinctiveness and strength** of the earlier mark — inherently distinctive or well-known marks enjoy broader protection
5. **Overall impression** — the global and holistic assessment, not a running score of individual factors

Courts draw on UK and EU case law. The analysis is impressionistic and holistic, not factor-by-factor.

### Infringement provisions (TMA s34)

| Section | Type | Test |
|---|---|---|
| s34(1)(a) | Identical/similar mark on identical/similar goods | Likely to cause deception or confusion |
| s34(1)(b) | Similar mark on similar goods | Likely to cause deception or confusion |
| s34(1)(c) | Dilution — well-known registered mark on any goods | Unfair advantage of, or detriment to, distinctive character or repute. No confusion required. |

## 4. Clearance beyond the register

A CIPC register search alone is insufficient for clearance. Additional checks are required:

| Risk source | How to check | Blocking right |
|---|---|---|
| Well-known unregistered foreign marks | Assess SA consumer awareness and online presence | TMA s35, passing off |
| Common-law passing off rights | Trade directories, social media, domain searches | Common-law delict |
| Company / close corporation names | CIPC entity name search | Name conflicts and confusion |
| Domain names (.co.za) | ZADNA / registry search | Domain dispute (not TM infringement but reputational conflict) |
| Market reputation / trade names | Market research, internet search, SA media archives | Passing off |

## 5. Well-known marks (TMA s35)

TMA s35 gives effect to South Africa's Paris Convention obligations (Article 6bis).

**Effect:** A mark entitled to protection under the Paris Convention as a well-known trade mark is protected in South Africa **even if it is not registered** and **even if the owner has no local business presence**, provided the mark is well known in the relevant sector of the SA public.

**What s35 enables:**
- Infringement actions against SA users of conflicting marks
- Opposition to registration of conflicting marks at CIPC
- Cancellation of existing registrations that conflict with the well-known mark

**Threshold:** The mark must be well known in SA among a substantial proportion of the relevant sector of the public. Global fame alone is not sufficient — there must be knowledge in South Africa. Online presence, advertising, and media coverage contribute to establishing this.

## 6. Passing off (common-law delict)

Passing off is a **common-law delict** protecting unregistered marks where the TMA does not apply. It is not a creature of statute.

### Three elements (all must be established)

| Element | What to show |
|---|---|
| **Reputation/goodwill** | The plaintiff has an established reputation and goodwill in the mark or get-up in SA at the date of the defendant's first use. Use evidence: years of use, sales figures, advertising spend, consumer recognition. |
| **Misrepresentation** | The defendant's use is likely to cause members of the public to believe the defendant's goods or services are those of, or are associated with, the plaintiff. Need not be intentional. |
| **Damage** | The misrepresentation causes or is likely to cause damage to the plaintiff's reputation or goodwill — lost sales, brand dilution, association with inferior goods. |

**Remedies:** Interdict (to restrain continued passing off) and damages (loss flowing from the passing off). Delivery up of infringing materials may also be ordered.

## 7. Defences (TMA s36)

| Defence | Statutory basis | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Own name use | TMA s36(1)(a) | Bona fide use of one's own name or the name of a predecessor in business |
| Descriptive use | TMA s36(1)(b) | Use of indications of kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, value, geographical origin, or other characteristics of goods or services |
| Intended purpose | TMA s36(1)(c) | Use of the mark to indicate the intended purpose of a product (e.g., spare parts, consumables, compatible products) |

All s36 defences require that the use be **bona fide** and in accordance with fair practice in industry or commerce. Bad-faith use of a competitor's mark as a "descriptor" will not be protected.

## 8. Duration and renewal

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Initial term | 10 years from filing date |
| Renewal | Indefinitely renewable in further 10-year periods |
| Pre-expiry filing | Renewal can be filed from 6 months before the expiry date |
| Grace period | 6-month grace period after expiry with a penalty fee |
| Non-use cancellation | After 5 years of non-use, a registration is vulnerable to cancellation on application to the High Court (TMA s27) |

## 9. What NOT to replicate from US practice

| US concept | Why it does not apply in SA |
|---|---|
| **Du Pont factors** (CCPA/CAFC) | US-circuit-specific multi-factor confusion test for USPTO oppositions. SA does not enumerate factors in this way. Apply the global appreciation test. |
| **Polaroid test** (2nd Circuit) | US circuit multi-factor confusion test. Not applicable in SA. |
| **Sleekcraft test** (9th Circuit) | US circuit multi-factor confusion test. Not applicable in SA. |
| **TTAB (Trademark Trial and Appeal Board)** | USPTO's administrative tribunal for oppositions and cancellations. SA uses CIPC opposition proceedings and High Court applications. |
| **Lanham Act §32 / §43(a)** | US statutory basis for trademark infringement and unfair competition. SA uses TMA s34 (registered marks) and common-law passing off (unregistered marks). |
| **Likelihood of confusion under Lanham Act** | US statutory formulation. SA uses TMA s34 with the global appreciation test. |
| **Federal registration symbol (®)** | In SA, ™ indicates an unregistered mark is claimed; ® may be used once a mark is registered at CIPC. Using ® without SA registration is misleading but not a distinct criminal offence under TMA. |
| **Priority based on use (common-law TM rights)** | SA is first-to-file. US recognises common-law priority from first use in commerce. SA recognises reputation-based passing off rights but not trademark priority from use alone. |
