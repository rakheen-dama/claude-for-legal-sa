# Copyright and Fair Dealing — South African Framework

This overlay covers SA copyright categories, subsistence, fair dealing (a closed list — not US fair use), ECTA s77 notice-and-takedown, duration, moral rights, and enforcement. Loaded by takedown, infringement-triage, and oss-review when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Categories of works (Copyright Act s2)

The Copyright Act 98 of 1978 protects **9 categories** of works. Copyright only subsists in these categories — there is no general copyright in ideas, information, or facts.

| # | Category | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Literary works | Includes novels, articles, letters, tables, compilations, and **computer programs** |
| 2 | Musical works | Musical notation, not sound recordings |
| 3 | Artistic works | Drawings, paintings, sculptures, photographs, engravings, works of architecture, works of craftsmanship |
| 4 | Cinematograph films | Moving images recorded on any medium |
| 5 | Sound recordings | Recorded onto any medium |
| 6 | Broadcasts | Radio and television broadcasts |
| 7 | Programme-carrying signals | Satellite signals carrying broadcast content |
| 8 | Published editions | The typographical arrangement of published editions of literary, musical, or artistic works |
| 9 | Computer programs | Expressly listed as a separate category (in addition to their inclusion in literary works) |

**Note on scope:** Copyright in a literary work (including a computer program) does not protect the underlying ideas, algorithms, or methods — only the expression. A program's functionality is not protected by copyright; only the specific code expressing it is protected.

## 2. Subsistence of copyright (Copyright Act s3-4)

**No registration required.** Copyright arises automatically on creation and fixation. There is no SA copyright registration system equivalent to the US Copyright Office. Registration is not a prerequisite to enforcement.

### Requirements for copyright to subsist

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Originality** | The work must originate with the author — not copied from another, and exhibiting at least a minimal degree of skill, labour, or effort (low threshold in SA, similar to UK approach) |
| **Qualified person** | The author must be a qualified person — an SA citizen or resident, or a person domiciled in an SA Berne Convention country — at the time the work is made |
| **First publication** | Where the author is not a qualified person, copyright subsists if the work is first published in SA or in another Berne Convention country |
| **Fixation** | Works must be reduced to material form (written, recorded, drawn, etc.) — ideas alone are not protected |

---

## 3. Duration of copyright

| Category | Term |
|---|---|
| Literary, musical, and artistic works | Life of the author + 50 years (from end of year of death) |
| Cinematograph films, sound recordings, broadcasts, programme-carrying signals | 50 years from the end of the year of first publication or first broadcast |
| Computer programs | Life of the author + 50 years |
| Works of unknown authorship | 50 years from end of year of creation or first publication |

**SA is life+50, not life+70.** The EU and US extended duration to life+70. SA has not enacted a similar extension. Works in the extended 20-year window (life+50 to life+70) that are protected in the US or EU may be in the public domain in SA.

## 4. Fair dealing (Copyright Act s12) — CLOSED LIST

**SA fair dealing is fundamentally different from US fair use. The permitted purposes are a CLOSED LIST.**

### Permitted purposes under s12

| Permitted purpose | Notes |
|---|---|
| Research or private study | Personal, non-commercial. Mass copying for institutional or commercial purposes is not covered. |
| Personal or private use | Personal and private — not commercial use or distribution |
| Criticism or review | Of the work or another work. Use must be for genuine critical or analytical purpose, not mere commercial extraction. |
| Reporting current events | News reporting of current events. Commentary on historical events is not "current." |

### What SA fair dealing does NOT permit

The following are **not** permitted purposes under the s12 closed list:

- Transformative use for creative or commercial purposes
- Parody (no standalone parody exception in current Act)
- Educational copying beyond personal study
- Archival or preservation copying beyond specific statutory provisions
- Use that "adds value" without serving one of the four listed purposes

**Critical operational rule:** A use that would be defensible as fair use under US 17 USC §107 may be an infringing use in SA if it does not fall within one of the four listed purposes. Do not apply the US four-factor test. Do not invoke "transformative use" as a defence in SA.

## 5. ECTA s77 notice-and-takedown

South Africa's online takedown mechanism is Chapter XI of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002 (ECTA), specifically **s77**. This is the SA equivalent of US DMCA §512, but with significant differences.

### Requirements for a valid s77 takedown notice

A complainant must send a notice to the **service provider's designated agent** containing:

| Required element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Full contact details of the complainant | Name, address, telephone number, email |
| Description of the infringing material | What the material is, what right it infringes |
| Precise location (URL) | Specific URL or location of the infringing material on the service provider's servers |
| Statement of ownership or authority | Statement that the complainant owns the right or is authorised to act |
| Statement that the information is accurate | Accuracy declaration |
| Signature | Handwritten or electronic signature of the complainant |

### Service provider safe harbour

Service providers that receive, act on, and comply with valid takedown notices benefit from **limitations on liability** under ECTA — the safe harbour protects against secondary infringement claims for hosting infringing content if the provider acts expeditiously on notice.

### No statutory counter-notice

**ECTA contains no mandatory counter-notice procedure.** Unlike DMCA §512(g), there is no statutory mechanism requiring the ISP to restore removed content if the alleged infringer submits a counter-notice. In practice:

- ISPs tend to **over-comply** (remove content promptly) to preserve their safe harbour protection
- Alleged infringers who dispute a takedown must pursue the complainant directly (demand retraction, or bring legal proceedings for unlawful removal)
- There is no DMCA-style "put-back" obligation

### ECTA vs DMCA: key comparison

| Feature | SA ECTA s77 | US DMCA §512 |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory basis | ECTA 25 of 2002 s77 | 17 USC §512 |
| Designated agent | Service provider appoints an agent | Service provider registers agent with Copyright Office |
| Counter-notice | No statutory procedure | Mandatory counter-notice procedure (§512(g)) |
| Put-back obligation | None | 10-14 business days unless court order |
| Scope | Any unlawful content (not copyright-limited) | Copyright infringement specifically |
| Perjury gate | Accuracy declaration required | §512(f) misrepresentation liability |

## 6. Infringement (Copyright Act s23)

### Direct infringement (s23(1))

Copyright is infringed by any person who, without the authority of the copyright owner, does or causes any other person to do **any act that the owner has the exclusive right to do or to authorise.** The exclusive rights include: reproducing, publishing, performing, broadcasting, transmitting, adapting, and translating the work.

### Secondary infringement (s23(2)-(3))

Secondary infringement includes importing, selling, letting, distributing, or possessing for trade purposes an article which the person **knows or ought reasonably to know** is an infringing copy. Knowledge (actual or constructive) is required for secondary infringement.

## 7. Civil remedies (Copyright Act s24)

| Remedy | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Interdict** | Primary remedy — prohibit continued infringement. Interim and final. |
| **Damages** | Compensatory. Actual loss suffered by the plaintiff. |
| **Reasonable royalty** | In lieu of proven damages — the licence fee the parties would have agreed in a hypothetical negotiation. |
| **Delivery up** | Infringing copies and implements for making them may be delivered up or destroyed. |
| **Account of profits** | Disgorgement of the infringer's profits attributable to the infringement, in lieu of damages. |

### Criminal penalties (Copyright Act s25-27)

Commercial copyright infringement is a criminal offence. Penalties include fines and imprisonment. Rights holders can lay criminal complaints in addition to pursuing civil remedies.

## 8. Moral rights (Copyright Act s20)

SA recognises moral rights for authors:

| Right | Description |
|---|---|
| **Paternity (authorship) right** | The author has the right to claim authorship of the work and to have the work attributed to them |
| **Integrity right** | The author has the right to object to any distortion, mutilation, or other modification of the work that would be prejudicial to the author's honour or reputation |

**Key rules:**
- Moral rights **cannot be assigned** — they remain with the author regardless of copyright transfer
- Moral rights can be **waived** by express written consent from the author
- Even where the employer owns copyright under s21(1)(d), the employee-author retains moral rights
- Include a moral rights waiver in all IP agreements and creative services contracts

---

## 9. Computer programs — special rules

- Computer programs are protected as **literary works** under s2 and are also listed as a separate category
- Authorship rules follow the same principles as literary works
- Where a program is created by an employee in the course of employment: employer owns copyright (s21(1)(d)) — no separate consideration needed
- Where a program is created by a contractor: **contractor owns copyright unless there is a written assignment** (s22(3))
- Computer-generated works: where there is no human author, the person who arranged for the creation of the work is deemed the author (s1, definition of "computer-generated")

## 10. What NOT to replicate from US practice

| US concept | Why it does not apply in SA |
|---|---|
| **DMCA §512 (safe harbour)** | SA uses ECTA Chapter XI s77 — a different statutory framework with no counter-notice and no designated agent registry equivalent to the US Copyright Office. |
| **Four-factor fair use test (17 USC §107)** | SA has fair dealing (s12) with a CLOSED LIST of permitted purposes. Do not apply purpose, nature, amount, and market-impact analysis as a general test. |
| **Transformative use doctrine** | There is no transformative use doctrine in SA copyright law. A transformative use that would be fair use in the US may be infringing in SA if it does not serve one of the s12 permitted purposes. |
| **Lenz v Universal "good faith" requirement** | The US case requiring good-faith consideration of fair use before sending a DMCA takedown has no SA equivalent. ECTA s77 notices require an accuracy statement but not a prior fair use analysis. |
| **Work made for hire (17 USC §101)** | US doctrine with specific categories of qualifying works. SA uses employer ownership (s21(1)(d)) — a different test based on employment relationship, not the nature of the work. See ip-ownership-and-clauses.md. |
| **Life+70 duration** | SA copyright lasts life+50. Do not assume US-duration works are still protected in SA. |
| **Copyright registration as prerequisite to sue** | In the US, registration is generally required to bring an infringement suit for works of US origin. SA has no registration system — copyright is automatic and enforceable without registration. |
| **Statutory damages (17 USC §504)** | SA has no statutory damages system. Plaintiffs must prove actual loss or elect an account of profits. |
