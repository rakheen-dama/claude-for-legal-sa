# Entity Compliance — CIPC Framework

This overlay covers entity registration, annual compliance, and the CIPC regulatory framework for South African corporate entities. It is loaded by entity-compliance when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. CIPC overview

The **Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC)** is the statutory body responsible for company registration and regulation in South Africa, established under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.

### Functions

- **Registrar of companies** — registration, deregistration, and reinstatement of companies.
- **Registrar of close corporations** — administration of existing CCs under the Close Corporations Act 69 of 1984.
- **Registrar of cooperatives** — registration under the Cooperatives Act 14 of 2005.
- **IP administration** — registration of trademarks, patents, designs, and copyright in cinematograph films.
- **Enforcement** — compliance monitoring, beneficial ownership register, and enforcement actions.
- **Annual returns** — receipt and processing of annual returns and financial accountability supplements.

### Contact

- Website: [cipc.co.za](https://www.cipc.co.za)
- BizPortal: online self-service portal for company registration, annual returns, and beneficial ownership declarations.
- E-services: electronic filing for most transactional forms.

## 2. SA entity types

| Type | Abbreviation | Governing law | Board required | Audit requirement | Annual return deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private company | (Pty) Ltd | Companies Act 71 of 2008 | Yes — at least 1 director (s66(2)) | Depends on PI score / Reg 28 | 30 BD after anniversary of incorporation |
| Public company | Ltd | Companies Act 71 of 2008 | Yes — at least 3 directors (s66(2)) | Mandatory audit | 30 BD after anniversary of incorporation |
| State-owned company | SOC Ltd | Companies Act 71 of 2008 | Yes — at least 3 directors (s66(2)) | Mandatory audit | 30 BD after anniversary of incorporation |
| Non-profit company | NPC | Companies Act 71 of 2008 | Yes — at least 3 directors (s66(2)) | Depends on PI score / Reg 28 | 30 BD after anniversary of incorporation |
| Personal liability company | Inc | Companies Act 71 of 2008 | Yes — at least 1 director (s66(2)) | Depends on PI score / Reg 28 | 30 BD after anniversary of incorporation |
| Close corporation | CC | Close Corporations Act 69 of 1984 | No — members manage collectively | Depends on turnover / Reg 28 | Anniversary month + 1 month |
| External company | — | Companies Act s23 | No SA board required | Depends on SA activities | 30 BD after anniversary of registration |

### Key distinctions

- **No new CCs** may be registered since 1 May 2011 (effective date of the Companies Act). Existing CCs may continue to operate under the CC Act indefinitely.
- **External companies** are companies incorporated outside SA that carry on business or are parties to employment in SA. They must register with the CIPC within 20 BD of establishing a place of business in SA (s23(1)).
- **Personal liability companies** (Inc) have the same structure as private companies, but directors are jointly and severally liable with the company for debts arising from the carrying on of the company's business (s19(3)). Commonly used for professional practices (attorneys, auditors).

## 3. Annual returns (s33)

### Deadline

Every company must file an annual return within **30 business days** after the **anniversary of its date of incorporation** (s33(1)). For close corporations, the deadline is the end of the month following the anniversary month.

### Filing method

Annual returns may only be filed **electronically** via the CIPC BizPortal. Paper filing is no longer accepted.

### Fee tiers

| Annual turnover | Filing fee | Late filing fee |
|---|---|---|
| Below R1 million | R100 | R150 |
| R1 million – R10 million | R450 | R600 |
| R10 million – R25 million | R2,000 | R2,500 |
| R25 million and above | R3,000 | R4,000 |

### Contents

The annual return must include:

1. **Beneficial ownership declaration** — mandatory since July 2024; the annual return cannot be filed without a current BO declaration.
2. **Annual financial statements (AFS)** or **Financial Accountability Supplement (FAS)** — depending on the entity's audit requirement.
3. **Compliance checklist** — confirmation that the entity has complied with Companies Act requirements during the reporting period.
4. **Director/member details** — confirmation that director or member information on the register is current.

### Consequences of non-filing

- Late filing incurs higher fees (see table above).
- Two successive years of non-filing triggers deregistration proceedings (s82).
- Reinstatement after deregistration is not automatic and requires a separate application and fee (R200).

## 4. Beneficial ownership declarations

### Background

South Africa's beneficial ownership regime was introduced to comply with **Financial Action Task Force (FATF)** recommendations and to support South Africa's efforts to exit the FATF grey list. The regime is implemented through the General Laws (Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing) Amendment Act 22 of 2022 and CIPC regulations.

### Requirements

| Event | Deadline |
|---|---|
| New entity registration | Within **10 business days** of incorporation |
| Change in beneficial ownership | Within **10 business days** of the change |
| Annual return filing | Must include current BO declaration |

### Beneficial owner definition

A beneficial owner is a natural person who, directly or indirectly:

- Owns **5% or more** of the shares or voting rights.
- Exercises or has the right to exercise **significant influence or control** over the entity.
- Has the right to appoint or remove directors.
- Has the right to receive distributions or other financial benefits.

### Hard stop

The CIPC BizPortal enforces a **hard stop** — an annual return **cannot be filed** unless a current beneficial ownership declaration is on record. This means entities that have not filed BO declarations are unable to file annual returns and will eventually be flagged for deregistration.

### Penalties

- **Criminal offence** for providing false or misleading information in a BO declaration.
- Directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable for non-compliance.
- Ongoing FATF grey-list pressure is driving increased CIPC enforcement of BO requirements.

## 5. AFS/FAS filing

### Audit requirement hierarchy

The audit requirement depends on the entity type and its **public interest score** (Reg 26):

| Entity type | Audit requirement |
|---|---|
| Public company (Ltd) | **Mandatory** audited AFS |
| State-owned company (SOC Ltd) | **Mandatory** audited AFS |
| Private company in fiduciary capacity | **Mandatory** audited AFS (Reg 29) |
| Private company with PI score > threshold | **Mandatory** audited AFS (Reg 28) |
| Private company with PI score ≤ threshold | Independently reviewed AFS **or** FAS (CoR30.2) |
| Close corporation | Depends on turnover and Reg 28 |

### Public interest score (Reg 26)

The PI score is calculated as:

- Number of employees at year-end, **plus**
- Third-party liability at year-end (in ZAR millions), **plus**
- Annual turnover (in ZAR millions), **plus**
- Number of individual persons with a beneficial interest in securities at year-end.

### Financial Accountability Supplement (FAS)

For entities below the audit threshold, the alternative to audited AFS is the **FAS** (Form CoR30.2). The FAS is a self-assessment form completed by the entity confirming basic financial information. It does not require independent verification.

### 2024 Amendment — access restrictions

The Companies Amendment Act 16 of 2024 introduced restrictions on third-party access to the AFS of private companies, personal liability companies, and NPCs with a PI score below the prescribed threshold:

- PI score below **100** (internally prepared AFS): third parties cannot access.
- PI score below **350** (independently prepared AFS): third parties cannot access.
- This change was motivated by concerns that small private companies were being unfairly exposed to public scrutiny of their financial information.

### Board approval

AFS must be:

1. **Approved by the board** of directors (or by members for a CC).
2. **Signed** by an authorised director.
3. Filed with the annual return.

## 6. Close corporation specifics

### Filing deadline

Close corporations file annual returns under CC Act s15A. The deadline is the **end of the month following the anniversary month** of registration.

### Fees

| Annual turnover | Filing fee | Late penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Below R50 million | R100 | R150 per late lodgment |
| R50 million and above | R4,000 | Additional penalties may apply |

### Governance

- **No board of directors** — members manage the CC collectively.
- A **members' agreement** governs internal relations between members (not mandatory but strongly recommended).
- Each member has a **percentage interest** in the CC (not shares).
- Members owe fiduciary duties to the CC under CC Act s42.

### Conversion to company

Existing CCs may voluntarily convert to a company by filing **Form CoR18.1**. Benefits of conversion:

- Access to the full Companies Act governance framework.
- Share capital flexibility (CC interests are percentage-based; companies can issue multiple classes of shares).
- Investor and funder expectations (many institutional investors and banks prefer the company form).
- Eligibility for listing (CCs cannot list on the JSE).

Conversion does not affect the CC's existing contracts, rights, or obligations — the CC continues as a company with the same registration number (prefixed differently).

### No new registrations

No new CC registrations have been permitted since **1 May 2011** (the effective date of the Companies Act). Existing CCs may continue to operate indefinitely under the CC Act, but all new entities must be registered as companies.

## 7. Deregistration (s82)

### Trigger

CIPC initiates deregistration proceedings when an entity has failed to file annual returns for **two successive years** (s82(3)).

### Process

1. CIPC places the entity in **"AR deregistration process"** status on the register.
2. CIPC issues a notice of intention to deregister (published on the CIPC website).
3. If the entity does not cure the default within the notice period, CIPC moves the entity to **"final deregistered"** status.

### January–February 2025 bulk action

In January and February 2025, CIPC conducted a bulk deregistration action affecting approximately **800,000 non-compliant entities** that had failed to file annual returns for two or more consecutive years. This was the largest mass deregistration in CIPC history.

### Consequences of deregistration

| Consequence | Detail |
|---|---|
| Loss of legal personality | The entity ceases to exist as a legal person |
| Bank accounts frozen | Banks freeze accounts upon receiving notice of deregistration |
| Director personal liability | Directors may become personally liable for debts incurred while the company was deregistered |
| Creditor refusal | Creditors may refuse to pay amounts owed to the deregistered entity |
| Service provider refusal | Service providers (auditors, attorneys) may refuse to act |
| Asset absorption | The state may absorb the assets of a deregistered company with no known claimants |
| Contract void | The entity can no longer enter into or enforce contracts |
| Litigation standing | The entity has no legal standing to institute or defend legal proceedings |

## 8. Reinstatement

### Application

An application for reinstatement of a deregistered entity is made on **Form CoR40.5**.

### Who may apply

- The company itself (through its last registered directors).
- An authorised representative of the company.
- A third party with a legitimate interest (e.g., a creditor, shareholder, or contracting party).

### Requirements

1. **Evidence of economic activity** — the applicant must demonstrate that the entity had economic activity or other economic value at the time of final deregistration.
2. **Reinstatement fee** — R200.
3. **Application form** — completed CoR40.5 with supporting documentation.

### Process

| Step | Action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | File CoR40.5 with CIPC | No statutory deadline (but promptly after deregistration) |
| 2 | CIPC reviews and approves (discretionary) | Variable — typically 2–6 weeks |
| 3 | Status changes to "reinstatement process" | Upon approval |
| 4 | File ALL outstanding annual returns and BO declarations | Within **30 business days** of reinstatement approval |
| 5 | Status changes to "active" | Upon filing of all outstanding returns |

### If outstanding returns not filed

If the entity fails to file all outstanding annual returns and BO declarations within the 30 business day window, the entity's status **reverts to "final deregistered"** and the reinstatement is void.

### Not guaranteed

Reinstatement is **discretionary** — CIPC is not obligated to approve the application. Factors considered include:

- Whether the entity had genuine economic activity at deregistration.
- Whether there are outstanding compliance issues beyond annual returns.
- Whether reinstatement would prejudice any third party.

## 9. CoR amendment forms

| Form | Purpose | When required |
|---|---|---|
| CoR39 | Change of directors or auditor | Within **10 BD** of the change |
| CoR21.1 | Change of registered office address | Within **10 BD** of the change |
| CoR15.2 | Amendment of MOI | After special resolution (≥75%) — filed within **10 BD** |
| CoR15.1A | New MOI (standard form) | Adoption of new standard-form MOI |
| CoR15.1B | New MOI (custom) | Adoption of new custom MOI |
| CoR18.1 | Conversion of CC to company | Voluntary — filed with CIPC |
| CoR25 | Restoration of company to register | After court order directing restoration |
| CoR40.5 | Reinstatement of deregistered entity | Voluntary application after deregistration |
| CoR44 | Change of company name | After special resolution and CIPC approval of new name |
| CoR40.1 | Voluntary deregistration | Company has no assets, no liabilities, not carrying on business |
| CoR14.1 | Notice of incorporation | New company registration |
| CoR14.1A | Notice of incorporation (short standard) | New company using short standard MOI |

### Filing method

All CoR forms are filed electronically via the CIPC BizPortal or e-services platform. Paper filing is no longer accepted for most forms.

### Common errors

- **Late CoR39 filing** — director changes must be filed within 10 BD. Late filing may result in the CIPC register reflecting incorrect directors, which creates confusion for third parties, banks, and due diligence processes.
- **MOI not filed after amendment** — a special resolution to amend the MOI is not effective until the CoR15.2 and amended MOI are filed with CIPC.
- **Name reservation expiry** — a company name reservation (CoR9.1) expires after **6 months**. If the company is not incorporated within that period, the name must be reserved again.

The following YAML structure is designed for use by the entity-compliance skill to track and alert on SA entity compliance deadlines:

```yaml
entities:
  - name: "Example Holdings (Pty) Ltd"
    registration: "2015/123456/07"
    type: "pty_ltd"
    anniversary: "2015-06-15"
    annual_return:
      status: "filed"
      last_filed: "2026-03-01"
      next_due: "2026-07-28"
    beneficial_ownership:
      status: "filed"
      last_filed: "2026-03-01"
    afs:
      type: "FAS"
      last_filed: "2026-03-01"
    directors:
      - name: "J. Smith"
        id_type: "SA ID"
    notes: "Below audit threshold (PI score < 100)"

  - name: "Example Trading (Pty) Ltd"
    registration: "2018/654321/07"
    type: "pty_ltd"
    anniversary: "2018-09-20"
    annual_return:
      status: "overdue"
      last_filed: "2024-10-15"
      next_due: "2025-10-31"
    beneficial_ownership:
      status: "overdue"
      last_filed: "2024-10-15"
    afs:
      type: "audited"
      last_filed: "2024-10-15"
    directors:
      - name: "A. Nkosi"
        id_type: "SA ID"
      - name: "B. van der Merwe"
        id_type: "SA ID"
    notes: "PI score > 350 — mandatory independent review or audit"

  - name: "Legacy Services CC"
    registration: "CK1999/012345/23"
    type: "cc"
    anniversary: "1999-03-01"
    annual_return:
      status: "filed"
      last_filed: "2026-04-15"
      next_due: "2027-04-30"
    beneficial_ownership:
      status: "filed"
      last_filed: "2026-04-15"
    afs:
      type: "FAS"
      last_filed: "2026-04-15"
    members:
      - name: "C. Govender"
        interest: "60%"
      - name: "D. Mokoena"
        interest: "40%"
    notes: "CC — consider conversion to (Pty) Ltd via CoR18.1"
```

### Field definitions

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `registration` | CIPC registration number (format: YYYY/NNNNNN/NN for companies; CK format for CCs) |
| `type` | Entity type: `pty_ltd`, `ltd`, `soc_ltd`, `npc`, `inc`, `cc`, `external` |
| `anniversary` | Date of incorporation — used to calculate annual return deadline |
| `annual_return.status` | `filed`, `overdue`, `deregistration_process`, `final_deregistered` |
| `annual_return.next_due` | Calculated: anniversary + 30 BD (companies) or end of anniversary month + 1 month (CCs) |
| `beneficial_ownership.status` | `filed`, `overdue`, `not_filed` |
| `afs.type` | `audited`, `independently_reviewed`, `FAS` |
| `directors` / `members` | Current directors (companies) or members (CCs) |
| `notes` | Free-text compliance notes |
