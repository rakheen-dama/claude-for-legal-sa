# Board Governance — South African Framework

This overlay covers corporate governance under the Companies Act 71 of 2008 — board authority, meetings, written resolutions, director duties and liability, statutory committees, and King IV. It is loaded by board-minutes and written-consent when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Board authority and structure

### Governing document

The company's **Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI)** is the sole governing document under the Companies Act. SA does not use bylaws, articles of association, or corporate charters. The MOI defines:

- The rights, duties, and responsibilities of shareholders, directors, and other persons.
- Any restrictions on the powers of the board (s15(2)(a)(iii)).
- The composition of the board and any requirements for director qualifications.

### Board authority

Section 66(1) provides that the business and affairs of a company must be managed by or under the direction of its board. The board has the authority to exercise **all of the powers and perform any of the functions of the company**, except to the extent that the Act or the company's MOI provides otherwise.

### Individual directors

Individual directors have no authority to bind the company unless:

- The MOI specifically grants such authority, or
- The board has passed a resolution delegating authority to the individual director.

A director who purports to bind the company without authority does not bind the company (subject to the Turquand rule and s20(7) protection of third parties dealing in good faith).

### Unitary board

South African law provides for a **unitary board structure** — there is no supervisory board or dual board structure. All directors (executive and non-executive) sit on a single board and bear the same fiduciary duties and duties of care and skill.

### King IV

The King IV Report on Corporate Governance for South Africa (2016) applies on an **apply-and-explain** basis. For JSE-listed companies, compliance with King IV is **mandatory** through the JSE Listings Requirements (para 3.84). For all other companies, King IV is voluntary but recommended as best practice.

## 2. Board meetings (s73)

### Calling a meeting

- Directors may collectively determine the time and place for meetings.
- **Any director** may call a board meeting at any time (s73(1)).
- The MOI may prescribe specific notice requirements; in the absence of MOI provisions, reasonable notice must be given.

### Quorum

The quorum for a board meeting is a **majority of the directors** (s73(5)), unless the MOI provides otherwise. The MOI may set a higher or lower quorum, but the quorum may not be less than the number required to ensure that at least one director is present if there are fewer than three directors.

### Voting

- Each director has **one vote** (s73(5)).
- A matter before the board is decided by a **majority of the votes** cast by directors present at the meeting.
- In the event of a tied vote, the chairperson does **not** have a casting vote unless the MOI specifically provides for one.

### Electronic participation

Section 73(3) permits directors to participate in board meetings by **electronic communication** (e.g., telephone or video conference), provided:

- All participants can communicate concurrently with each other without intermediate persons.
- The MOI does not prohibit electronic participation.

Participation by electronic means constitutes attendance at the meeting for purposes of quorum and voting.

### Minutes

- The company must keep minutes of all board meetings (s73(4)).
- Minutes are **prima facie evidence** of the proceedings and of any resolution passed.
- Minutes must be entered in the company's minute book and be available for inspection.

## 3. Round-robin resolutions (s74)

### Resolutions without a meeting

Section 74 permits directors to take decisions **other than at a meeting** by way of written resolution (commonly called "round-robin" resolutions).

### Requirements

1. **Signed by a majority** of directors (or the number specified in the MOI if different) — s74(1).
2. **Effective** when the last required signature is obtained.
3. **Same legal effect** as a resolution passed at a properly constituted board meeting.
4. **Multiple copies** — each signatory may sign a different copy of the resolution; the copies together constitute the resolution.
5. **Minutes book** — the resolution must be entered in the company's minutes book.

### MOI restrictions

The MOI may **restrict or prohibit** the use of round-robin resolutions (s74(2)). Where the MOI imposes restrictions (e.g., requiring unanimity instead of a majority, or prohibiting round-robins for specific types of decisions), those restrictions must be observed.

### Practical considerations

- Round-robin resolutions are commonly used for administrative or routine matters where calling a full board meeting is impractical.
- For contentious matters, a board meeting is preferable to allow discussion and debate.
- Electronic signatures are accepted under the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002, subject to the requirements of that Act.

## 4. Shareholder resolutions without meeting (s60)

### Written consent in lieu of meeting

Section 60 permits shareholders to adopt resolutions without holding a meeting, by way of written consent.

### Requirements

1. **Quorum** — the shareholders who consent must collectively hold sufficient voting rights to constitute a quorum for a meeting at which the resolution could have been voted on.
2. **Resolution threshold** — the written consents received must meet the threshold for the type of resolution:
   - **Ordinary resolution**: >50% of the voting rights exercised (s65(7)).
   - **Special resolution**: ≥75% of the voting rights exercised (s65(9)).
3. **MOI restrictions** — the MOI may restrict or prohibit the use of written consent resolutions.

### Practical use

- The most practical application is **unanimous written consent** by all shareholders, which avoids any quorum or threshold calculation issues.
- For companies with a single shareholder, written consent is the standard method for passing resolutions.
- For special resolutions (e.g., MOI amendments per s16, name changes, fundamental transactions), the ≥75% threshold applies to **all voting rights**, not just those exercised, making written consent impractical unless a very high percentage of shareholders participate.

### Effective date

The resolution becomes effective on the date on which the last required written consent is received, or on a later date specified in the resolution.

## 5. Director duties (s76)

### Partially codified duties

The Companies Act partially codifies director duties in s76 but does **not** abolish common law duties. Directors are therefore subject to both statutory and common law obligations.

### Statutory duties

**Section 76(2) — no misuse of position or information:**

A director must not:
- Use the position of director, or any information obtained while acting as director, to gain an advantage for themselves or for another person other than the company or a wholly-owned subsidiary.
- Knowingly cause harm to the company or a subsidiary.

**Section 76(3) — standards of conduct:**

A director must exercise the powers and perform the functions of director:

| Duty | Standard |
|---|---|
| (a) Good faith | Act in good faith and for a proper purpose |
| (b) Proper purpose | Exercise powers for the purpose for which they were conferred |
| (c) Best interests | Act in the best interests of the company |
| (d) Care, skill, and diligence | Exercise the degree of care, skill, and diligence that may reasonably be expected of a person carrying out the same functions, having the general knowledge, skill, and experience of that director |

The standard in s76(3)(d) is both **objective** (reasonable person carrying out the same functions) and **subjective** (having the knowledge, skill, and experience of that particular director). A director with specialist knowledge (e.g., an accountant or lawyer) is held to a higher standard in relation to their area of expertise.

### Business judgment rule (s76(4))

A director satisfies the obligations of s76(3)(b) and (c) if the director:

1. Took reasonably diligent steps to become informed about the matter.
2. Had no personal financial interest in the matter (or complied with s75).
3. Had a rational basis for believing, and did believe, that the decision was in the best interests of the company.

### Common law duties (not abolished)

- Duty not to exceed the powers conferred by the MOI or the Act.
- Duty to exercise unfettered discretion (no fettering by contract or otherwise).
- Duty not to misappropriate corporate opportunities.
- Duty to account for secret profits.
- Duty of confidentiality.

## 6. Personal financial interests (s75)

### Declaration obligation

A director who has a **personal financial interest** in respect of a matter to be considered at a meeting of the board must:

1. **Declare** the nature and extent of the interest **at each meeting** at which the matter is to be considered, **before** the matter is considered.
2. The declaration must be made to the board (not privately to the chairperson).

### Recusal requirement

An interested director:

- May **not attend** the portion of the meeting during which the matter is considered (unless required to constitute a quorum, in which case the director may attend but may **not vote**).
- May **not vote** on the matter under any circumstances.

### "Personal financial interest"

Defined in s1 as a direct material interest of the director, of a financial, monetary, or economic nature, or to which a monetary value may be attributed. This includes interests of **related persons** (as defined in s2), such as:

- Spouse or domestic partner.
- Close family members.
- Entities controlled by the director or related persons.

### Consequences of non-compliance

| Consequence | Detail |
|---|---|
| Void resolution | Board resolution may be declared void or voidable if an interested director voted and the vote was material to the outcome |
| Personal liability | Director is liable to the company for any loss, damages, or costs sustained as a result of the breach (s77(2)(a)) |
| Delinquency | Repeated or material failure to comply may ground a delinquency application (s162) |

### Ongoing duty

The duty to declare a personal financial interest is an **ongoing duty** — it is not discharged by a single declaration. If the matter comes before the board at a subsequent meeting, the director must declare the interest again at that meeting.

## 7. Director liability (s77)

### Statutory liability

**Section 77(2)** — a director is liable to the company for any loss, damages, or costs sustained by the company as a consequence of:

- A **breach of a fiduciary duty** arising from s75 (personal financial interests), s76 (standards of conduct), or any other provision of the Act.
- A breach of any provision of the Act.
- Any act or omission that amounts to a breach of the director's fiduciary duties at common law.

**Section 77(3)** — a director is liable for any loss, damages, or costs sustained by the company as a direct or indirect consequence of the director having:

- Acted in the name of the company, signed anything on behalf of the company, or purported to bind the company despite knowing that they lacked the authority to do so.
- Acquiesced in the carrying on of the company's business in a reckless manner, or with intent to defraud.
- Signed, consented to, or authorised financial statements that are materially false or misleading.

### Joint and several liability

Section 77(6) provides that where two or more directors are liable under s77, their liability is **joint and several**. This means the company (or a shareholder suing derivatively) may pursue any one director for the full amount of the loss.

### 2024 Amendment

The Companies Amendment Act 16 of 2024 introduced two significant changes (effective 27 December 2024):

1. **Extended prescription** — a court may extend the 3-year prescription period for claims against directors on good cause shown.
2. **Delinquency period** — the period for bringing a delinquency application under s162 has been extended to **5 years** after the person ceases to be a director (previously 2 years). This extension applies **retrospectively**.

### Indemnification and insurance (s78)

- A company **may** indemnify a director for liability incurred in the capacity of director, except where the liability arises from wilful misconduct or wilful breach of trust (s78(5)).
- A company **may** purchase insurance to protect a director against liability (s78(7)).
- The MOI may provide for indemnification or insurance, and the company may agree to indemnify by contract.

## 8. Audit committee (s94)

### Mandatory for

An audit committee is mandatory for:

- **Public companies** (Ltd)
- **State-owned companies** (SOC Ltd)
- Companies with a **public interest score** above the prescribed threshold (Reg 28)

### Composition

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Minimum members | 3 |
| Director status | All members must be **non-executive** directors |
| Independence | A **majority** of members must be independent |
| Qualifications | At least one member must have relevant qualifications or experience in finance, accounting, or auditing |

### Election

Audit committee members are **elected by shareholders** at each annual general meeting (s94(2)). This is a deliberate departure from the position in many other jurisdictions where the board appoints audit committee members — the shareholder election requirement is intended to enhance the committee's independence from management.

### Functions

Section 94(7) prescribes the following functions:

1. **Nominate** for appointment as auditor a registered auditor who is independent of the company.
2. **Determine** the auditor's terms of engagement and fees.
3. **Ensure** that the appointment of the auditor complies with the Act, the Auditing Profession Act 26 of 2005, and any applicable requirements.
4. **Report** to shareholders at the AGM on matters within its mandate.
5. **Receive and deal with** any concerns or complaints relating to accounting practices, internal audit, the content or auditing of financial statements, internal financial controls, or any related matter (including reportable irregularities under the Auditing Profession Act).
6. **Make submissions** to the board on any matter concerning the company's accounting policies, financial controls, records, and reporting.

### Overlap restriction

Members of the audit committee may **not** simultaneously serve on the social and ethics committee.

## 9. Social & ethics committee (s72(4), Reg 43)

### Mandatory for

A social and ethics committee is mandatory for:

- **Listed public companies**
- **State-owned companies** (SOC Ltd)
- Companies with a **public interest score of ≥500** in any two of the previous five financial years (Reg 43(1))

### Composition

**Pre-2024 amendment:** At least 3 members, including at least one director.

**2024 amendment (effective 27 December 2024):** For public companies and state-owned companies, a **majority** of members must be **non-executive directors** who have not been involved in the day-to-day management of the company's business in the previous **3 financial years**.

### Prescribed monitoring functions (Reg 43(5))

The committee must monitor the company's activities with regard to:

1. **Social and economic development** — including:
   - B-BBEE compliance (B-BBEE Act 46 of 2013)
   - Employment equity (Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998)
   - UN Global Compact principles
   - OECD recommendations on corruption

2. **Good corporate citizenship** — including:
   - Promotion of equality, prevention of unfair discrimination, and reduction of corruption
   - Environmental, health, and public safety contributions
   - Consumer relationships
   - Community development and sponsorship

3. **Environment, health, and public safety** — including:
   - Impact of the company's activities on the environment, health, and safety
   - Compliance with environmental legislation (NEMA, NWA, NEMWA)

4. **Consumer relationships** — including:
   - Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 compliance
   - Advertising, public relations, and compliance with consumer protection laws

5. **Labour and employment** — including:
   - Standing of the company in terms of the ILO Protocol on decent work
   - Educational development of employees

### Reporting

The committee must report to shareholders at each **annual general meeting** on the matters within its mandate. The report must describe the activities of the committee during the financial year.

### Exemption

A company may apply to the **Companies Tribunal** for an exemption from the requirement to establish a social and ethics committee. An exemption, if granted, is valid for a period of **5 years**.

## 10. King IV governance principles

### Overview

The King IV Report on Corporate Governance for South Africa (2016) replaces King III (2009) and establishes **17 governance principles** organised around four governance outcomes:

1. **Ethical culture** — the governing body should lead ethically and effectively.
2. **Good performance** — the governing body should govern the strategy, performance, and reporting of the organisation.
3. **Effective control** — the governing body should ensure adequate and effective controls.
4. **Legitimacy** — the governing body should ensure the organisation is a responsible corporate citizen.

### Application

| Company type | King IV status |
|---|---|
| JSE-listed | **Mandatory** via JSE Listings Requirements (para 3.84) |
| State-owned companies | **Mandatory** via PFMA and DPE governance framework |
| Private companies | **Voluntary** but recommended as best practice |
| Non-profit companies | **Voluntary** — King IV includes sector supplement |

### Key recommendations for corporate-legal practice

**Board composition:**

- **Majority non-executive** — the board should comprise a majority of non-executive members, the majority of whom should be independent.
- **Lead independent director** — where the chairperson is not independent, a lead independent director should be appointed.
- **Independence assessment** — a director who has served for more than **9 years** should be subject to a rigorous independence assessment. The recommended minimum cool-off period before a former executive can be considered independent is **3 years**.

**Diversity:**

- The board should set targets for race and gender diversity and report against those targets.
- Skills diversity should be considered — the board should collectively possess the skills, knowledge, and experience relevant to the company's business.

**Separation of roles:**

- The **chairperson** and the **CEO** should not be the same person.
- The chairperson should be an independent non-executive director.

**Board evaluation:**

- The board should conduct a formal evaluation of its performance and that of its committees and individual directors at least every **2 years**.
- An external facilitator should be used at least every **3 years**.

**Integrated reporting:**

- King IV promotes integrated reporting — the annual integrated report should provide a holistic view of the organisation's performance and its ability to create value in the short, medium, and long term.

## Committee comparison

| Feature | Audit Committee (s94) | Social & Ethics Committee (s72(4)) | Remuneration Committee (King IV) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mandatory for | Public, SOC, high-PI-score | Listed public, SOC, PI ≥500 | JSE-listed (King IV / JSE LR) |
| Min members | 3 | 3 (at least 1 director) | 3 (King IV) |
| Composition | All non-exec, majority independent | Majority non-exec (2024 amendment) | Majority non-exec, majority independent |
| Chair | Independent non-exec | Director or prescribed officer | Independent non-exec |
| Key function | Auditor nomination, financial oversight | Social/economic/environmental monitoring | Remuneration policy, director pay |
| Election | By shareholders at AGM | By board | By board |
| Statutory basis | Companies Act s94 | Companies Act s72(4), Reg 43 | King IV (no statutory basis) |
| Reporting | To shareholders at AGM | To shareholders at AGM | Remuneration policy & report now statutory for public/SOC (ss30A/30B) |

> **2026 update — statutory remuneration regime (ss30A, 30B, 30(4)-(6)).** From **22 May 2026** (Companies Amendment Act 16 of 2024, commenced by Proclamation 313 of 2026, GG 54722), remuneration governance for **public and state-owned companies** is no longer a King IV-only, non-binding-vote matter:
> - **s30A** — the company must table a **remuneration policy** for shareholder approval by **ordinary resolution** at the AGM; once approved it stands for three years, and material amendments need fresh approval.
> - **s30B** — the company must table an annual **remuneration report** (background statement + policy + implementation report) for approval by ordinary resolution. The implementation report must disclose each director's and prescribed officer's total remuneration, the highest- and lowest-paid employee, the average and median employee remuneration, and the pay gap between the top 5% and bottom 5%. A **two-strike** consequence follows non-approval.
> - **s30(4)-(6)** — for **any company whose AFS must be audited**, the annual financial statements must now disclose remuneration and benefits of **each individual director and prescribed officer, by name**.
