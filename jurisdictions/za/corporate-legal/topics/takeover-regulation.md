# Takeover Regulation — South African Framework

This overlay covers Companies Act Chapter 5 Parts B and C — the Takeover Regulation Panel (TRP), mandatory offers, squeeze-outs, and deal mechanics for affected transactions. It is loaded by closing-checklist and diligence-issue-extraction when jurisdiction = ZA.

---

## 1. Regulatory framework

The Takeover Regulation Panel (TRP) is established under s196 of the Companies Act 71 of 2008 as an independent regulatory body. Its authority derives from s119 and extends to the regulation of any **affected transaction** or **offer** without regard to the commercial advantages or disadvantages of the transaction.

### Objectives

The TRP's regulatory objectives are:

1. **Fairness** — ensuring that all holders of securities of the same class are treated similarly and are afforded a fair opportunity to participate in the benefits of any affected transaction.
2. **Market integrity** — maintaining confidence in the integrity of the securities market.
3. **Equal information** — ensuring that all holders of relevant securities receive sufficient information to reach a properly informed decision.

### Compliance certificate

Section 119(4) requires that any affected transaction or offer may not be implemented unless the TRP has issued a **compliance certificate** confirming that the transaction has been conducted in compliance with the Act and the Takeover Regulations. A transaction implemented without a compliance certificate is void.

### TRP powers

- Issue compliance certificates or refuse to do so.
- Exempt transactions from Part B and Part C requirements (s119(6)).
- Investigate complaints relating to affected transactions.
- Apply to court for enforcement of its rulings.
- Compel disclosure of information from any party to an affected transaction.

## 2. Regulated companies (s118)

The TRP's jurisdiction applies only to transactions involving **regulated companies**. Three categories of company fall within the TRP's jurisdiction:

### (a) Public companies

All public companies (Ltd) are regulated companies. This includes companies listed on the JSE as well as unlisted public companies.

### (b) State-owned companies

All state-owned companies (SOC Ltd) are regulated companies, regardless of size or shareholder composition.

### (c) Private companies meeting the threshold

A private company is a regulated company if, in the **24 months** immediately before the date of a particular affected transaction, **more than 10%** of the issued securities of any class have been transferred between persons who were not related to each other at the time of transfer.

### 2024 Amendment (NOT YET IN FORCE)

The Companies Amendment Act 16 of 2024 introduces a revised threshold for private companies. Once in force, a private company will be regulated if it has:

- **10 or more shareholders**, AND
- Its annual turnover or asset value exceeds a threshold to be determined by the Minister.

Until the Minister publishes the financial threshold and the amendment commences, the existing 10% transfer test continues to apply.

### MOI opt-in

A company that does not fall within any of the three categories above may voluntarily subject itself to TRP regulation by including a provision in its MOI (s118(1)(c)(ii)).

### TRP exemption

The TRP may exempt any company from Part B or Part C requirements on application (s119(6)), if the TRP is satisfied that it is not necessary in the public interest to apply those provisions.

## 3. Affected transactions (s117)

Section 117(1)(c) defines seven categories of affected transactions:

| # | Transaction type | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | Disposal of all or greater part of assets or undertaking (s112) of a regulated company | s112 read with s117(1)(c)(i) |
| (ii) | Amalgamation or merger involving a regulated company (s113) | s113 read with s117(1)(c)(ii) |
| (iii) | Scheme of arrangement of a regulated company (s114) | s114 read with s117(1)(c)(iii) |
| (iv) | Acquisition of a beneficial interest in voting securities of a regulated company (s122(1)) | s122(1) |
| (v) | Announced intention to acquire remaining voting securities of a regulated company | s117(1)(c)(v) |
| (vi) | Mandatory offer (s123) | s123 |
| (vii) | Compulsory acquisition / squeeze-out (s124) | s124 |

Any transaction falling within these categories triggers the full suite of TRP compliance requirements, including the obligation to obtain a compliance certificate before implementation.

## 4. Mandatory offer (s123)

### 35% threshold

A mandatory offer is triggered when a person, alone or together with any **concert parties**, acquires a beneficial interest in sufficient securities of a regulated company to bring their total holding to **35% or more** of the voting rights.

### Obligation

Within **1 business day** of crossing the 35% threshold, the acquiring party must notify all remaining holders of voting securities and make an offer to acquire all of their remaining securities.

### Consideration

- The cash consideration must be at or above the **highest price paid** by the offeror (or concert parties) for securities of the same class during the **previous 6 months**.
- A **bank guarantee or escrow** is required to demonstrate the offeror's ability to pay (Reg 111(4)).

### Creeping threshold (35–50%)

If the offeror already holds between 35% and 50% of the voting rights, acquiring an additional **more than 5%** in any **12-month period** triggers a new mandatory offer obligation.

### Exceptions

| Exception | Condition |
|---|---|
| Shareholder waiver | >50% of independent shareholders approve a waiver (for share issues, cash subscriptions, or rights offers only) |
| TRP exemption | TRP grants exemption under s119(6) |
| Non-voting preference shares | Excluded from the calculation of voting rights |
| Involuntary acquisition | Threshold crossed due to actions of others (e.g., share buy-back by the company) — no obligation if offeror did not initiate |

## 5. Squeeze-out (s124)

### Trigger

If an offer made under Part B or Part C is accepted by holders of at least **90%** of the securities to which the offer relates (excluding securities already held by the offeror), within **4 months** of the offer being made, the offeror may exercise compulsory acquisition rights.

### Compulsory acquisition

The offeror may give notice to the remaining (non-accepting) shareholders that it intends to acquire their securities on the **same terms** as the original offer. The notice must be given within **2 months** after the 4-month acceptance period.

### Dissenting shareholder rights

A dissenting shareholder who receives a squeeze-out notice may apply to court within **30 business days** for:

- An **injunction** preventing the compulsory acquisition, or
- An order that the acquisition proceed on **modified terms** (e.g., a higher price).

### If no court application

If no dissenting shareholder applies to court within the 30 business day period, or if the court dismisses the application, the offeror acquires the shares on the original offer terms. The consideration payable to shareholders who cannot be found must be deposited with an appropriate authority.

## 6. Acting in concert

### Definition

Persons act in concert when they cooperate pursuant to an **agreement, arrangement, or understanding** (whether formal or informal, express or tacit) to **obtain or consolidate control** of a regulated company.

### Disclosure

- **Form TRP 84** must be filed whenever a concert party arrangement exists, disclosing the identity of all parties and the nature of the arrangement.
- Holdings of all concert parties are **aggregated** for purposes of calculating the 35% mandatory offer threshold.

### Risk: inadvertent concert party status

Shareholders who collaborate on board strategy, co-nominate directors, or coordinate voting on resolutions may inadvertently create concert party status. Key risk factors:

1. **Aggregation** — even without any share acquisition, the combined holdings of concert parties may exceed 35%.
2. **Disclosure obligation** — even if the 35% threshold is not crossed, the existence of a concert party arrangement must be disclosed on Form TRP 84.
3. **Trigger risk** — once concert party status is established and aggregate holdings exceed 35%, the acquisition of even **one additional share** by any member of the concert party triggers a mandatory offer obligation for all remaining shares.

### Clarification

Coming into concert with another party when the **aggregate** of the parties' holdings already exceeds 35% does **not** by itself trigger a mandatory offer. The obligation is triggered only when the concert parties **acquire additional shares** after the concert party arrangement is established.

## 7. Frustrating action restrictions (s126)

### When restrictions apply

The restrictions apply when the board of the target company:

- **Believes** that a bona fide offer is imminent, or
- Has **received** a bona fide offer.

### Prohibited actions

Without both **TRP written approval** and **shareholder approval**, the target board must not:

| # | Prohibited action |
|---|---|
| (a) | Take any action that could effectively frustrate the offer or deny shareholders the opportunity to decide on its merits |
| (b) | Issue any authorised but unissued shares or convertible securities |
| (c) | Grant any options or similar rights over shares |
| (d) | Sell, dispose of, or acquire material assets outside the ordinary course of business |
| (e) | Enter into any contract outside the ordinary course of business |
| (f) | Make any distribution that is abnormal as to amount or timing |

### Pre-existing obligation defence

Actions taken pursuant to an obligation that existed **before** the board became aware of the offer are not subject to the restrictions, provided the obligation was entered into in the ordinary course of business and was not created in anticipation of the offer.

### Effect on defensive measures

The frustrating action provisions effectively **prohibit poison pills** in South Africa. Unlike in the US where target boards may deploy various defensive measures (poison pills, white knights, crown jewel defences), the SA framework requires the target board to:

- Consider **all** offers on their merits, including unsolicited and hostile offers.
- Not take any action that prevents shareholders from deciding for themselves.
- Obtain TRP and shareholder approval before any defensive measure.

## 8. Disclosure requirements (s122)

### Beneficial interest notifications

| Threshold | Disclosure obligation | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | Disclose to target company | Within 3 BD of crossing |
| 10% | Disclose to target company | Within 3 BD of crossing |
| 15% | Disclose to target company | Within 3 BD of crossing |
| Each further multiple of 5% | Disclose to target company | Within 3 BD of crossing |

The target company must **announce** all such notifications to all shareholders.

### MOI-imposed thresholds

A company's MOI may impose **stricter** notification thresholds (e.g., 2% or 3%), but may not impose thresholds more lenient than the statutory minimums.

### During offer period

Once an offer has been announced, additional disclosure requirements apply:

1. **Daily disclosure** — all parties with an interest in the outcome of the offer must disclose all share dealings on a daily basis.
2. **Director conflict disclosure** — directors of both the offeror and the target must disclose any conflict of interest (Reg 106(3)).
3. **Associates and concert parties** — dealings by associates and concert parties must also be disclosed.

### Non-compliance

Failure to comply with disclosure requirements may result in:

- TRP refusing to issue a compliance certificate.
- Votes attached to undisclosed holdings being suspended.
- Criminal prosecution for false or misleading disclosure.

## 9. Independent expert opinion

### Requirement

The **independent board** (the board of the target company, excluding any director who is not independent in relation to the transaction) must obtain an opinion from an **independent expert** in respect of any affected transaction.

### Fair and reasonable assessment

The independent expert must express an opinion on whether the offer is:

1. **Fair** — the consideration offered equals or exceeds the fair value of the securities. Fair value is typically determined by reference to a discounted cash flow valuation, comparable company analysis, and/or sum-of-the-parts valuation.

2. **Reasonable** — having regard to other qualitative factors (including but not limited to the offeror's future plans for the target, conditions attached to the offer, and whether a higher offer is likely), the offer should be accepted by shareholders.

### Split opinion

A split opinion is possible and common:

| Assessment | Meaning | Typical shareholder guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Fair **and** reasonable | Consideration equals/exceeds fair value and qualitative factors support acceptance | Accept |
| Fair **but not** reasonable | Consideration meets fair value but qualitative factors weigh against acceptance | Consider carefully |
| **Not** fair **but** reasonable | Consideration below fair value but qualitative factors (e.g., no better offer likely) support acceptance | Consider carefully |
| **Not** fair **and not** reasonable | Consideration below fair value and qualitative factors weigh against acceptance | Reject |

### Independent board's role

The independent board must:

- Consider the independent expert's opinion.
- Express its own view to shareholders on whether the offer should be accepted.
- Consider competing offers before recommending implementation.
- Disclose any changes in its recommendation and the reasons for such changes.

## 10. Offer mechanics

### Timetable

| Stage | Minimum period |
|---|---|
| Offer open period | 30 business days |
| Extension after revision of terms | 14 business days |
| Squeeze-out notice period | Within 2 months after 4-month acceptance period |

### Break fee cap

TRP Guideline 1/2013 imposes a **maximum break fee of 1%** of the total offer value. This applies to both inducement fees (payable by the target to the offeror if the target withdraws) and reverse break fees (payable by the offeror to the target if the offeror withdraws).

### Cash confirmation

- The offeror must provide an **irrevocable bank guarantee** or place cash in **escrow** sufficient to cover the full consideration payable (Reg 111).
- **No financing condition** is permitted — the offer must be **fully funded** at the time it is made. This is a critical difference from many other jurisdictions where offers may be conditional on obtaining financing.

### Cooling-off period

If an offer fails or is withdrawn, neither the offeror nor its concert parties may make a **new offer** or trigger a **mandatory offer** for the same target for a period of **12 months**.

### Post-close price parity

After the offer closes or becomes unconditional, the offeror may not offer more favourable terms for the target's securities for a period of **6 months**.

### Irrevocable undertakings

TRP Guideline 1/2013 limits irrevocable undertakings to a **maximum of 5 minority shareholders** each holding **≥5%** of the target's issued shares. This prevents the offeror from locking up the result before minority shareholders have an opportunity to consider the offer on its merits.
